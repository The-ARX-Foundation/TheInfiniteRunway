Nations should pass laws to preserve any remaining wilderness areas in their natural state, even if these areas could be developed for economic gain.

"If all insects dissapeared off the face of the earth, in 50 years all life would cease to exist. If all humans dissapeared off the face of the earth, in 50 years life would flourish." We as humans have the ability and thus responsability to take care of our planet, lest we lead ourselves to our own destruction.
I mostly support for the following two reasons, though concede that if sufficient requirement for use of the land and effective countermeasures for the problems created by its use can be presented there can be outliers to the sentiment. 

As our population bolsters we are rapidly increasing the rate of consumption of natural resources beyond that which is continuously sustainable. Deforrestation and the depletion of fossil fuels risk calamities such as global warming and massive supply chain failures. With this in mind, we must set our own limits on our consumption, otherwise we risk passing the point of no return. Specifically in the case of deforrestation, motions to prevent the removal of wilderness areas in their natural state are a significant step towards preventing the issues at hand. However, similar motions towards replantation similarly can prevent decline thus further supporting requirements may be needed.

One such requirement is that of ecological preservation; in the case of deforrestation, countless species are displaced upon the removal of their habitats. Not only does this cause significant disruption to their behavioral patterns, it often leads to the diminishing of the species population --even extinction in some cases-- as their food supplies are subsequently displaced or even removed entirely. One could ask, "why does this matter to us humans?", to which I remind them that the sustenance we ourselves depend on depends on a very delicate balance of ecological factors that would be impacted by our continuous interferance with that ecosystem. Similarly, from a preservation mindset, we take the natural state from both the residents of the wilderness, but future humans. If only for being able to experience what our planet was like prior to our intervention, the cultural relevance of the wilderness alone should prompt one to reconsider its removal.

If there is any case in which there is significant evidence towards the requirement of the use of the land in particular and that effective countermeasures can be put in place (i.e. replantation of all native flora and relocation of native fauna) I find it difficult to barr the use of the land, however feel that pure economic benefit is not enough to satisfy the condition of significant evidence.

We all live on this planet, not just humans but the many species that cohabitate this place we call home. As we are faced with the choice between greed and prosperity for all we must take into account not just the fates of ourselves but that of those who cannot make the choice themselves. It is imperative that we preserve any remaining wilderness areas in their natural state for as long as we can, otherwise we risk losing it all."
