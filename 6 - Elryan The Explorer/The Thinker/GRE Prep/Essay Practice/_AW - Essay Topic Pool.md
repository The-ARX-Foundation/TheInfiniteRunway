# Big Buckets (with ~counts)

- **Education & Curriculum** (~35)  
    Admissions, required breadth, teacher pay, grading competition, group work, electives vs requirements, job-oriented majors.
    
- **Government/Policy & Law** (~25)  
    Experts vs public will, laws’ limits, national curricula, step-down rules, common ground vs ideals, risk-taking policies.
    
- **Science/Research & Technology** (~18)  
    Funding unknown-consequence research, prioritizing public benefit, beginners vs experts, tech helping/hurting learning & interaction.
    
- **Ethics & [[Philosophy]]** (~20)  
    Ends justify means, self-interest vs altruism, imagination vs knowledge, nature of facts, success metrics, truth vs image.
    
- **Society, Culture & Human Nature** (~22)  
    Pace of life, smartphones & social skills, privacy erosion, [[Boredom]], determinants of behavior, youth trends, role models/heroes.
    
- **Leadership & Governance** (~15)  
    Ethics for leaders, responsiveness to opinion, being shaped by demands, skepticism of new leaders.
    
- **Environment & Arts Funding** (~10)  
    Wilderness preservation, trash limits, endangered species, arts vs basic needs, government funding of arts.
    
- **History, Knowledge & Epistemology** (~7)  
    Value of history for decisions, complexity with knowledge growth, learning from agreement vs disagreement.
    
- **Cities & Urbanism** (~5)  
    Cities as cultural engines, old buildings vs development, funding major cities.
    
- **Work, Economy & Corporations** (~5)  
    Corporate social responsibility, jobs-plentiful fields, efficiency/leisure tradeoffs.

# Smart Practice Sets (6 prompts each)

## Set A — “Abstract + Balanced”

1. As we acquire more knowledge, things become more complex and mysterious.
    
2. Claim: Any “fact” should be mistrusted; much “factual” info proves inaccurate.
    
3. No act is purely for others; all actions stem from self-interest.
    
4. The best test of an argument is convincing someone with the opposite view.
    
5. We learn more from those we agree with than from those we don’t.
    
6. [[The Human]] mind will always be superior to machines.
    

## Set B — “Policy & Tradeoffs”

1. Governments should not fund research with unclear consequences.
    
2. Nations should suspend arts funding when citizens are hungry/unemployed.
    
3. A nation should require all students to study the same national curriculum.
    
4. Towns should impose strict trash limits per household.
    
5. Laws should be flexible to circumstances, times, places.
    
6. Politicians should pursue consensus over elusive ideals.
    

## Set C — “Education — Nuance Required”

1. Teachers’ salaries should be based on student performance.
    
2. Educational institutions should dissuade students unlikely to succeed.
    
3. Group assignments should replace much lecture-based instruction.
    
4. Universities should require courses outside one’s field.
    
5. College majors should be chosen based on job availability.
    
6. Educators should assess explanations of ideas, not recall of facts.
    

# How to use this

- **Rotate** sets A → B → C across three sessions.
    
- For each prompt: 2-min outline → 24-min draft → 4-min revision.
    
- After writing, **sketch the best counter-case** you didn’t take (1–2 minutes).
    
- Keep an **example bank** (policy, history, science, literature) and reuse flexibly.
