Laws restricting the freedom of speech are sometimes necessary in a democratic society.  
  
Write a response in which you discuss the extent to which you agree or disagree with the statement and explain your reasoning for the position you take. In developing and supporting your position, you should consider ways in which the statement might or might not hold true and explain how these considerations shape your position.

The first amendment to the Bill of Rights grants American citizens the right of freedom of speech. This freedom was stated to be unalienable, meaning that it should not and therefore could not be taken away from those who call the country home. The prompt states that laws restricting the freedom of speech are sometimes necessary in a democratic society, a sentiment I mostly disagree with for for the following two reasons. However, I do concede that deterrants on aggregious utilization of the freedom of speech can be put in place if the consequences of that speech warrant it.
Firstly, when speech is limited, the effective flow of democracy is impeded. For example, if speech regarding criticism towards government officials were restricted, the public would have no way to voice their opinions and incite change where needed. Similarly in authoritarian states such as that of North Korea and China, where censorship is rampant, the citizens are unable to truly participate in their governance. Citizen representation is the heart of democracy, if this is hindered then democracy itself is impeded.
Secondly, speech itself can often be misconstrued, likely leading to issues of implementation of laws. For example, satirical publications such of that of The Onion and Whitest Kids You Know often border on risque regarding political events, however are for the purpose of humor and the highlighting of issues the authors deem relevant. Supporting the potential for misuse of the law, the act of cancel culture has shown the capability of speech to be taken out of context. It is well within the realm of possibility that this could be utilized purposefully to target for the use of slander. Speech can often be misappropriated, thus blanket laws on speech itself could prove deletrious, even harmful to implement.
In the case that speech harms another citizen, however, a contest of rights occurs; that of the speaker to say what they say and that of the recipient to their peace. For example, hate speech and other derogatory utilizations of speech can often stigmatize and alienate minorities. Similarly, verbal threats --regardless of the intention of follow-through-- present the victim with the the conclusion of a violation to their safety. In both scenarios, although only intangible damage has occurred, the psychological impacts of the speech require repremand lest they become everpresent. The restriction of the speech outright is not the answer, however, though consequences for the utilization of such speech do not constitute restrictions outright and may be applicable. The effects of the speech must be what is focused on, not the speech itself.
In order for democracy to flourish, speech must remain unadulterated. The people must be able to say what needs to be said for proper representation to occur. Any law that focuses on speech would additionally likely be difficult if not deleterious to implement. While there are ways for speech to be harmful, the speech itself should not be the focus of the law, rather its effects.
