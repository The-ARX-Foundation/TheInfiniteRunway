Themes: [[Philosophy]]

**Claim:** No act is done purely for the benefit of others.  
**Reason:** All actions — even those that seem to be done for other people — are based on self-interest.

_Write a response in which you discuss the extent to which you agree or disagree with the claim and the reason on which that claim is based._


The claim draws a hard line with the word "purely", denoting that there is no conceivable scenario in which an action occurs without any driving factor than the benefit of others. This claim is substantiated by the reasoning that even actions that seem to be done for others are based on self-interest, a common sentiment I myself held until further consideration led me to the concept of self-sacrifice. 

The trolley problem is one of my favorite intuition pumps for philosophical discussions, entailing a scenario in which a lever controlling an oncoming trolley's trajectory towards one of two paths is available to the subject to utilize to switch which path the trolley takes. With a slight twist I feel this anecdote quite apt: the subject is on the tracks with someone they hold dear on the other set. In this scenario, pulling the lever to divert the trolley from the loved one towards the subject cannot be definitively stated to have any other motivation than that of preventing the trolley from utilizing the track of the loved one, disregarding the cost of their own life (save the edge case of the subject pulling the lever for no reason at all). A more realistic example of this same scenario is in that of a disaster situation; say a mother saves their child at the cost of their own life. In both scenarios, the outcome of the loved one / child's survival is not one that the subject will be able to experience under the assumption of finality at [[Death]], thus cannot ultimately be stated that self-interest is at play. Potential counters to this include that of the self being genetic continuity or that of memory, but say the child is not blood related, too young to remember the subject, there are no witnesses to the event, etc. Regardless of the semantics, a scenario can be painted as an outlier to the claim, thus we cannot fully accept the claim to be true.

There is another line of thought that bridges onto predeterminism however. Unless an action is completely random, prompted by nothing within one's mind, it cannot be stated to be purely for any single reason. Neurologically / psychologically it is commonly accepted that we are a product of our development, and thus the reasons behind decisions become significantly complex when viewed through the light of these materialist perspectives. On that front I would have to agree that there is the possibility for an inkling of any prompting for action to be not purely for another's benefit, however it cannot be argued that there is not the possibility of one such event to occur; the culmination of all of one's development to the single life ending decision outlined prior for example.

While the sentiment of the claim is commonly accepted, the presence or even possibility for an outlier determines that the binary classification of the statement to be false ad infinitum.
