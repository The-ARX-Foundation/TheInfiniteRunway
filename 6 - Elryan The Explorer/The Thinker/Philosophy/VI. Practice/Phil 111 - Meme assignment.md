### Why Does Natural Law View Sex This Way?
The [[Natural Law]] argument that sex should be primarily for procreation comes from a **teleological** (purpose-driven) view of nature, especially as developed by **Aristotle** and later **Aquinas**. It holds that everything in nature has an **intrinsic purpose**, and for sex, that purpose is reproduction. Under this reasoning, any use of sex that **frustrates its natural end** (procreation) is considered morally disordered.

### Why Doesn't Natural Law Consider Bonobos?
Many animals, including bonobos, engage in **non-procreative** sex. However, traditional [[Natural Law]] theorists typically don't see animal behavior as morally relevant to human ethics. The reasoning is that:

1. **Humans Have Rational Souls** – Unlike animals, humans have the capacity for reason and moral responsibility. Just because something occurs in nature doesn’t make it morally right for humans (e.g., animals kill rivals, but murder is still wrong for us).
2. **Nature is Interpreted Teleologically** – Natural law isn’t about what _happens_ in nature but what fulfills a thing’s _purpose_. Since the biological function of sex is reproduction, anything that deliberately contradicts that function (like contraception or same-sex acts) is seen as unnatural.

### But is This View Rigid?
There are **modern critiques** of this strict interpretation. Some argue that sex also serves important **social and emotional functions**, not just reproduction. Even within marriage, sex can strengthen intimacy and express [[Love]], which some modern thinkers see as equally "natural" purposes.

Ultimately, **natural law’s rigidity comes from its assumption that there is a single, fixed purpose for sex**—but as pointed out by the meme, nature itself suggests a much more diverse picture.
