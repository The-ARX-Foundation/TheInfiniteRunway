## Is the reversal or loss of intelligence a kind of death?

If intelligence and sentience are the defining traits of personhood, then losing them should be seen as a kind of [[Death]]—perhaps not biological, but existential. If a cat enhanced with intelligence is reverted back, we are not merely returning it to its prior state but actively stripping away its ability to think, plan, and engage in the world as a person.

This parallels cases (like Flowers for Algernon as previously stated) where intelligence loss isn't just a cognitive shift but the destruction of an identity. What differentiates us from animals we consider lesser—livestock, pets, wildlife—if not our sentience? If a purely physicalist view holds, then all that makes us ‘human’ is in our skull, not an intangible soul. If this is true, then reducing intelligence is a form of [[Death]].

McPherson’s critique of consent as the sole moral foundation and Estes’ focus on mutual respect challenge us to consider whether we have an obligation to sustain intelligence where it exists. If abortion is debated in terms of denying a ‘future like ours,’ then is reducing intelligence equivalent to robbing an existing being of that future? Would it ever be ethical to strip an entity of personhood once granted? Conversely (as stated within my own packback question earlier this week) if there is intrinsic value in personhood itself would we not have an obligation to spread it in the first place? (Parallels to morality of bringing human life into this world and begging the question of if there is more to personhood than just the intelligence if it can be granted; see AI for example).
