Metaphysics isn’t without its share of paradoxes and challenges. Here are a few to test your growing understanding:

- **Zeno’s Paradoxes**  
    Zeno of Elea created paradoxes to challenge our understanding of motion and change. For example, in the **Paradox of the Arrow**, Zeno argues that for an arrow to reach its target, it must first reach the halfway point. But before it can reach that point, it must reach the halfway point to the halfway point, and so on—leading to an infinite number of tasks before the arrow can reach its destination.
    
- **The Problem of Causality**  
    **David Hume** questioned the very notion of cause and effect. He argued that we never actually perceive causality; we only observe one event following another. The idea that one event _causes_ another may just be a habit of thought, not a real [[Connection]] in nature.
    
- **The Nature of Time**  
    Is time real or merely a concept of the mind? If time is real, is it continuous, or is it composed of discrete moments? Philosophers like **Augustine of Hippo** questioned the reality of time, claiming that the past no longer exists, the future does not yet exist, and the present is but a fleeting, infinitesimal moment.
