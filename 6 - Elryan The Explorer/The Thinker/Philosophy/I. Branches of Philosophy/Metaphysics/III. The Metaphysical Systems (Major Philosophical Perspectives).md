Themes: [[Philosophy]]

Platonism
Plato’s metaphysical system asserts that the true reality lies in an abstract realm of perfect Forms. The physical world is a flawed copy of these ideal Forms. For example, all physical chairs are imperfect copies of the perfect Form of a chair that exists in the world of Forms.

Aristotelian Metaphysics
Aristotle rejected Plato’s realm of Forms, focusing instead on substances in the material world. For Aristotle, everything is a combination of form and matter. The form of a thing is its essence (what makes it what it is), and its matter is what it’s made of.

Leibniz’s Monadology
Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz proposed that reality consists of an infinite number of simple substances called monads. These are not physical objects but metaphysical points that embody the universe in miniature. According to Leibniz, the universe is the harmonious unfolding of these monads in pre-established harmony.

Kantian Transcendental Idealism
Immanuel Kant attempted to bridge the gap between rationalism and empiricism by suggesting that we never perceive the world "as it is" (the noumenal world). Instead, we perceive the world only as it appears to us (the phenomenal world), filtered through the lens of our mental faculties like space and time.
