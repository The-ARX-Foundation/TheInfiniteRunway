The foundations of logic rest on a few key concepts:

1. **Propositions (Statements)**: These are declarative sentences that can be either true or false. For example:
    - "All humans are mortal" (a true statement).
    - "The sky is green" (a false statement).
2. **Arguments**: An argument is a series of propositions where some propositions (premises) are meant to support another proposition (the conclusion). A **valid argument** is one where, if the premises are true, the conclusion must also be true.
3. **Validity and Soundness**:
    - **Validity** concerns whether the logical form of an argument ensures that the conclusion follows from the premises.
    - **Soundness** goes further: an argument is sound if it is both valid and the premises are actually true.
4. **Syllogisms**: A logical form that dates back to Aristotle, consisting of two premises leading to a conclusion. Example:
    - Premise 1: All humans are mortal.
    - Premise 2: Socrates is a human.
    - Conclusion: Socrates is mortal.

Mastering these elements allows one to construct and evaluate the strength of any argument.
