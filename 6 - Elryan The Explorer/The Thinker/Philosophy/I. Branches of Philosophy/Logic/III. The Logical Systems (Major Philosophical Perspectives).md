As the study of logic evolved, various systems of logic emerged, each expanding the scope of reasoning:

1. **Classical Logic (Aristotelian)**: Focuses on deductive reasoning using categorical propositions and syllogisms. It forms the basis for many other logical systems.
2. **Propositional Logic**: Deals with the logical relationships between whole propositions using connectors like "and," "or," "if...then." For instance, if "It is raining" is true, and "I am outside" is true, then "I am getting wet" logically follows.
3. **Predicate Logic (First-Order Logic)**: Expands propositional logic by including quantifiers like "all" (∀) and "some" (∃), allowing more detailed statements about individual objects and their properties.
4. **Modal Logic**: Deals with notions of necessity and possibility. It explores statements like "It is necessary that all humans are mortal" versus "It is possible that it will rain tomorrow."
5. **Non-Classical Logics**: Such as **paraconsistent logic**, which allows for handling [[Contradictions]] without collapsing the logical system, and **fuzzy logic**, which deals with degrees of truth rather than binary true/false statements.

Each system offers tools to address different kinds of reasoning, from mathematical proofs to philosophical debates on possibility and necessity.
