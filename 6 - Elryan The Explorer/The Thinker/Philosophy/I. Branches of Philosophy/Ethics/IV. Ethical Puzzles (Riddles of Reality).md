Themes: [[Philosophy]]

Ethics presents us with numerous puzzles and moral dilemmas that challenge our understanding of right and wrong:

1. **The Trolley Problem**: A classic moral dilemma where one must decide whether to pull a lever to divert a runaway trolley, saving five people but sacrificing one. It highlights the tension between utilitarian ethics (maximizing lives saved) and deontological principles (the wrongness of actively causing harm).
2. **Moral Relativism vs. Moral Absolutism**: Are there universal moral truths that apply to all humans, or are ethical norms culturally relative? This debate forces us to confront how we judge right and wrong across different societies and traditions.
3. **Moral Luck**: Can we hold individuals morally responsible for outcomes beyond their control? This puzzle challenges the very notion of moral responsibility, especially in cases where people’s actions lead to unforeseen consequences.
4. **The Problem of Evil**: In the realm of ethics and the [[Philosophy]] of religion, how can the existence of evil be reconciled with the idea of a benevolent, all-powerful deity? This is a profound moral and metaphysical puzzle.

These ethical puzzles push us to explore the limits of our moral reasoning and refine our ethical systems to account for complexity.
