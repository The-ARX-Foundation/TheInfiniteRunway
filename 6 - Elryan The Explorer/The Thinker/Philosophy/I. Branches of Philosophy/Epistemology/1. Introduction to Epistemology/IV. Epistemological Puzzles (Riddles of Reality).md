Themes: [[Philosophy]]

Epistemology poses several challenging puzzles that stretch the boundaries of our understanding:

1. **The Gettier Problem**: A challenge to the classical definition of knowledge as justified true [[Belief]]. **Edmund Gettier** presented scenarios where individuals have justified true beliefs that still seem to lack true knowledge due to luck. This has led to a re-evaluation of what it means to truly _know_ something.
2. **The Problem of Induction**: Introduced by **David Hume**, this puzzle questions how we can justify inductive reasoning (i.e., making generalizations based on particular observations). Just because the sun has risen every day in the past does not guarantee that it will rise tomorrow, highlighting the uncertainty of empirical knowledge.
3. **The Brain in a Vat**: A modern version of **Descartes' evil demon** thought experiment, this puzzle asks how we can be certain of the external world. If we were just brains in vats, being fed false experiences, how would we know? It raises questions about the reliability of perception and the possibility of skepticism.
4. **The Paradox of the Meno (Plato)**: How can we learn something if we do not already know it? If we know something, learning is unnecessary, but if we don’t know it, how can we recognize it when we encounter it? This paradox challenges the very process of acquiring knowledge.

These puzzles push the limits of epistemology, forcing us to reconsider how we justify knowledge and whether certainty is possible.
