### 🧠 1. Introduction to the Human Brain (14 min)

**Big picture:** Orientation tour of the whole brain.

- **Lobes & borders:**
    
    - _Frontal_ (motor, planning, speech), _Parietal_ (somatosensory, spatial), _Temporal_ (hearing, memory), _Occipital_ (vision).
        
    - _Central sulcus_ separates frontal ↔ parietal; _lateral (Sylvian) fissure_ separates temporal ↔ frontal/parietal; _parieto-occipital sulcus_ marks posterior limit.
        
- **Gray vs white:** Cortex = gray; inner tracts (internal capsule, corpus callosum) = white.
    
- **Functional localization:** Each cortical area has a dominant role, e.g. left-hemisphere language dominance, temporal-lobe memory.
    
- **Frontal section demo:** Reveals caudate, thalamus, internal capsule, and distinction between gray nuclei and white matter tracts.
    

**Takeaway:** You should now mentally visualize lobar functions and be able to orient to major sulci in a specimen or MRI.

### 🧫 2. The Normal Unfixed Brain (6 min)

**Purpose:** Show real tissue characteristics before fixation.

- **Texture:** Soft, gelatinous, easily deformed; highlights need for cranial protection and CSF cushioning.
    
- **Meninges visible:** Thin pia & arachnoid glistening on surface; tough opaque dura separate.
    
- **CSF protection:** Acts as hydraulic cushion within subarachnoid space.
    
- **Vessels:** Clean arteries on ventral surface (no atherosclerosis in specimen); demonstration of basilar, vertebral, and Circle of Willis beginnings.
    

**Takeaway:** Appreciate the brain as a fragile organ suspended in fluid and protected by meninges—not a dense “solid” organ.

### 🧭 3. Orientation: Planes of the Brain (8 min)

**Goal:** Translate 3-D anatomy into imaging planes.

- **Directional terms:**
    
    - _Anterior ↔ Posterior_, _Superior ↔ Inferior_ (hemispheres)
        
    - _Rostral ↔ Caudal_, _Dorsal ↔ Ventral_ (brainstem—note the 90° bend).
        
- **Planes:**
    
    - _Frontal/Coronal_ (ear-to-ear cuts)
        
    - _Axial/Transverse_ (horizontal slices)
        
    - _Sagittal_ (midline split).
        
- **Ventricular orientation:** Recognize lateral ventricles in coronal cuts and their continuity with third and fourth ventricles.
    

**Takeaway:** You should now be able to mentally rotate the brain and predict which structures appear in each imaging plane.

---

### 🩸 4. The Meninges (15 min)

**Purpose:** Protective layers & clinical relevance.

- **Layers & spaces:**
    
    - _Epidural_ (potential space) → hemorrhage from meningeal arteries.
        
    - _Subdural_ → bridging veins; crescent-shaped bleeds.
        
    - _Subarachnoid_ → true space with CSF & vessels.
        
- **Dural folds:** Falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli separate compartments; tentorial notch = potential herniation site.
    
- **Clinical demos:**
    
    - _Subdural hematoma_ specimen (venous bleed).
        
    - _Ruptured aneurysm_ and subarachnoid hemorrhage example.
        
    - Relationship of brainstem compression → uncal & tonsillar herniations.
        
- **Venous sinuses & granulations:** Superior sagittal sinus drains via arachnoid granulations into venous system.
    

**Takeaway:** Know meningeal layers, sinuses, and how raised intracranial pressure or bleeding patterns localize lesions.
