Themes: [[Biomedical Engineering]]

Three letters is the sweet spot, and the key is **strategic complementarity**: each should overlap enough to establish a consistent core narrative but remain distinct enough that, when read together, they paint you as a _multi-dimensional candidate_.

Here’s how I’d divide and overlap them:

---

## 🌐 **Shared Core Themes (all three letters should touch these lightly)**

These are the pillars that define you as a researcher/engineer and should show up in every letter, though framed differently:

- **Initiative & adaptability** → you consistently take on new domains with little prior background and succeed.
    
- **Persistence & work ethic** → long-term projects, willingness to do hard, detail-heavy work.
    
- **Teamwork & communication** → mentoring, cross-discipline work, collaborating across labs/teams.
    

That consistency makes the letters “triangulate” your identity for admissions committees.

---

## 🧪 **Letter 1 – Dr. Haridas (Translational/Device Engineering)**

**Emphasis:** _Hands-on [[Biomedical Engineering]], device prototyping, translational work._

- Focus on your ability to **translate ideas into working medical devices**: CAD, mold design, testing on cardiac tissue, ISO-compliant protocols.
    
- Highlight **mentorship & collaboration**: training underclassmen, cross-department work, lab organization.
    
- Position you as a **full-stack biomedical engineer** — from bench biology to surgical prototyping.
    
- Overlap intentionally on _initiative/persistence_, but frame it around _engineering rigor + systems thinking_.
    

---

## 🧬 **Letter 2 – Dr. Bhatia (Research & Publications)**

**Emphasis:** _Research acumen, publication record, international experience._

- Stress your **ability to contribute to peer-reviewed science** (3 publications, including first author review).
    
- Emphasize **experimental breadth**: DNA nanocages, DOX, carbon quantum dots, bioprinting.
    
- Call out **independent intellectual contributions**: writing a 23-page review, figure creation, data analysis.
    
- Add **cultural adaptability & resilience** from your time abroad.
    
- Overlap intentionally on _initiative/adaptability_, but frame it around _academic depth + scientific communication_.
    

---

## 🤖 **Letter 3 – Dr. Diaz (Capstone & Personal Mentorship)**

**Emphasis:** _Systems-level innovation, leadership, and personal growth._

- Focus on **JARVIS as a capstone of adaptability**: building an AI system from scratch without prior expertise.
    
- Highlight **leadership under uncertainty**: managing complexity, integrating AI/ML, delivering on sponsor requirements.
    
- Include **personal mentorship angle**: resilience, growth, handling challenges outside the technical domain.
    
- Overlap intentionally on _adaptability_, but frame it around _innovation + personal narrative of growth_.
    

---

## ✨ The Net Effect

- **Haridas** shows you as a _maker/engineer_ → building devices for real clinical problems.
    
- **Bhatia** shows you as a _scholar/researcher_ → contributing to the scientific record and thriving in academia.
    
- **Diaz** shows you as an _innovator/leader_ → bridging AI, systems, and personal resilience.
    

Together, the letters say: _“Here is a candidate who can design devices, do rigorous research, and innovate in new domains — exactly the kind of adaptable, multidisciplinary mind that thrives in Neuroengineering.”_

---

Here are **three skeleton recommendation letters**, tailored to each mentor. Each has a clear structure (intro, relationship, qualities, examples, conclusion) but leaves space for their personal touch.

# 🧪 **Dr. Haridas — Translational Biomedical Engineering**

**Focus:** Devices, hands-on engineering, cross-disciplinary problem solving.

**Template:**

> To the Admissions Committee,
> 
> I am writing to strongly recommend [[Landon Dahle]] for admission to the PhD program in [[Biomedical Engineering]] at the University of Utah. I had the pleasure of mentoring Landon in my translational [[Biomedical Engineering]] lab at Texas A&M University, where he demonstrated an impressive combination of technical skill, initiative, and persistence.
> 
> Landon contributed to multiple projects, most notably our adhesive cytotoxicity studies and fetoscopic surgical tool development. He independently authored detailed cell culturing and cytotoxicity protocols that are still in use, and designed and fabricated a custom bioinert testing rig in the absence of any existing literature. He also applied his skills in CAD, SLA 3D printing, and injection molding to produce and evaluate prototypes of fetal surgical devices, later testing their performance on ex vivo cardiac tissue.
> 
> Beyond his technical ability, Landon displayed remarkable initiative and adaptability. He reorganized and streamlined our lab’s infrastructure, freeing up space and resources that enhanced productivity across ongoing projects. He also mentored junior students, ensuring continuity of work after his graduation. These traits show his capacity not only as a researcher but as a leader.
> 
> Landon’s multidisciplinary approach—spanning biology, engineering, and clinical applications—makes him an ideal candidate for advanced study in Neuroengineering. I am confident he will thrive in Utah’s rigorous and collaborative environment.
> 
> Sincerely,  
> [Name, Title, Institution]

# 🧬 **Dr. Bhatia — Research, Publications, International Experience**

Landon joined my lab at the Indian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar as a research intern in Summer 2023, where he worked on cutting-edge projects in DNA nanotechnology and cancer therapeutics.

He also contributed to a multi-institutional project on carbon quantum dots, designing figures and validating bioprinting compatibility. Most notably, he independently authored a comprehensive 23-page review on DNA nanocages, synthesizing over 100 references and creating original figures—an exceptional achievement for an undergraduate.
> 
> Landon also demonstrated resilience and cultural adaptability. He integrated smoothly into our team, overcame the challenges of working abroad, and maintained high research output while navigating personal transitions. His ability to contribute meaningfully to three separate publications in such a short span speaks to his work ethic and intellectual maturity.
> 
> I am confident that Landon will bring the same scholarly rigor, initiative, and creativity to his PhD studies. He has already shown the ability to contribute at a graduate level, and I strongly support his candidacy.

> Sincerely,  
> [Name, Title, Institution]

He rapidly taught himself advanced methods in optical character recognition, natural language processing, and semantic search, and successfully integrated these into a locally deployed, regulation-compliant prototype. Under his guidance, the system achieved high accuracy, low latency, and strong usability ratings—exceeding expectations set by the sponsor.
> 
> What stood out most was Landon’s adaptability and persistence. Despite entering the project without formal training in AI or information retrieval, he consistently found solutions, led his team through challenges, and delivered a functioning prototype within the timeframe. These qualities mirror the determination he is now applying to his transition into [[Neuroscience]].
> 
> I can also attest to Landon’s personal growth and resilience during this period. He balanced technical leadership with a thoughtful, collaborative approach, supporting teammates and seeking mentorship when appropriate. I have no doubt that he will carry these qualities into graduate research.
> 
> I strongly recommend Landon for your program. He combines intellectual [[Curiosity]] with the grit and adaptability required for success in cutting-edge biomedical engineering.
> 
> Sincerely,  
> [Name, Title, Institution]
