### 🔬 Ender 3 → DIY Bioprinter Fabrication

**Situation:** The lab sought an affordable alternative to expensive bioprinters for tissue engineering in low-resource settings.  
**Task:** Adapt a consumer 3D printer (Ender 3) to print hydrogel-based scaffolds.  
**Action & Result (bullets):**

- Designed and modified a consumer-grade Ender 3 printer into a functional low-cost bioprinter, reducing system cost by over 90% compared to commercial alternatives.
    
- Engineered a DIY temperature-regulated enclosure using metal panels, a thermometer, and a hairdryer to maintain thermal stability during print tests.
    
- Conducted MCNB (methacrylated chitosan nanocomposite bioink) printability trials across variable temperatures to optimize resolution and extrusion behavior.
    
- Presented weekly findings to PIs and graduate mentors, integrating iterative feedback to refine system capabilities and ensure reproducibility.
    
- Co-authored publication validating bioprinting system for implementation in underfunded or remote research contexts.

### ⚙️ EnvisionTEC Bioplotter Refurbishment

**Situation:** The lab’s commercial bioprinter was out-of-warranty and unusable due to hardware failures and lack of documentation.  
**Task:** Restore functionality without vendor support or manuals.  
**Action & Result:**

- Reverse-engineered core printer functions using software tooltips and empirical testing, enabling lab use of equipment despite lacking documentation.
    
- Repaired system using 3D-printed and third-party replacement parts, saving an estimated $8,000+ in vendor service and extending equipment lifecycle.
	- Bringing back a $300k investment from the brink of disposal
    
- Enabled comparative studies between DIY and commercial bioprinters for hydrogel printability analysis and scaffold fidelity assessment.
    
- Restored capacity for in-gel bioprinting (e.g., alginate/collagen blends), expanding lab’s biomaterial experimentation range.

### 🧪 MCNB Bioink Printability & Rheological Characterization

**Situation:** The lab was developing a novel dual-crosslinkable bioink (MCNB) with thermo- and photo-responsive properties for advanced tissue engineering.  
**Task:** Evaluate the printability and rheological behavior of MCNB using custom and commercial bioprinting systems within budget constraints.  
**Action & Result:**

- Built a custom bioprinter from scratch using an Ender 3 base, aluminum extrusions, and DIY components, cutting costs by >90% compared to commercial systems and enabling rapid iteration on MCNB printability.
    
- Designed and fabricated a PID-regulated temperature enclosure using PVC sheeting, thermometers, aluminum framing, a hair dryer, and a timer relay system, enabling thermal environment control within ±1.5°C of target LCST range.
    
- Performed printability optimization at variable temperatures (25°C to 40°C) to identify critical MCNB extrusion conditions and nozzle stability for fine-featured scaffold printing.
    
- Conducted oscillatory rheometry tests using a TA Instruments Discovery Hybrid Rheometer (DHR), characterizing shear-thinning behavior, gelation kinetics, and LCST shift due to NB functionalization.
