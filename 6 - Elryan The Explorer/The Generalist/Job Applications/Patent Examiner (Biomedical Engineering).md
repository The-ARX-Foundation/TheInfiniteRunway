I believe that protecting intellectual property is not just a technical task, but a civic duty that sustains American innovation and economic freedom. 4. How has a strong work ethic contributed to your professional, academic or personal achievements? Provide one or two specific examples, and explain how those qualities would enable you to serve effectively in this position. (limit 1,200 characters)

To me, a solid work ethic is all about persistence, precision, and reliability, especially when the tasks get tough or monotonous. While I was at the Haridas Lab, I spent over six months maintaining mammalian cell cultures to assess long-term biocompatibility, ensuring that every experiment was sterile and reproducible. Achieving that level of consistency required a lot of attention to detail, daily discipline, and thorough documentation; skills that are essential for fairly and thoroughly evaluating patent claims. At The Medicine Shoppe, I compounded custom medications with an accuracy of ±5µg and processed hundreds of prescriptions every day while also managing DEA-regulated inventory. Juggling speed and accuracy under pressure really strengthened my commitment to patient safety and regulatory compliance. These experiences have shown me that diligence isn’t just about occasional bursts of effort; it’s about maintaining a steady commitment. As a Patent Examiner, I would bring that same work ethic to reviewing applications by ensuring that decisions are clear, impartial, and reliable, upholding the integrity of the USPTO and fostering innovation.
