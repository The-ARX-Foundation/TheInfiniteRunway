## 🧿 **[[Visual system]]**

The **visual system** is the sensory subsystem responsible for **detecting, processing, and interpreting light**. It is a complex integration of optical structures, neuronal pathways, and cortical areas that together produce the experience of **sight**.

### 🔍 **Primary Functions**

- **Light detection**
    
- **Image focusing**
    
- **Color and motion perception**
    
- **Depth and spatial awareness**
    
- **Visual reflexes and motor coordination**
    

---

### 👁️ **[[Eyes]]**

The primary sensory organs for vision. Light enters through the anterior structures and is focused onto the [[Retina]].

Key structures include:

- [[Cornea]]
    
- [[Pupil]]
    
- [[Iris]]
    
- [[Lens]]
    
- [[Retina]]
    

---

### 🔬 **[[Retina]] & [[Photoreceptors]]**

The retina is a thin layer of neural tissue that lines the back of the eye. It contains two major types of photoreceptor cells:

- [[Rods]] — highly sensitive to low light; enable night vision and grayscale perception.
    
- [[Cones]] — responsible for color vision and high-acuity detail, concentrated in the [[fovea]].
    

---

### 🔁 **[[Visual Pathway]]**

Light-initiated signals are converted into neural impulses and transmitted to the brain through the following pathway:

- [[Optic nerve (Cranial Nerve II)]]
    
- [[Optic chiasm]] — partial crossover of visual information
    
- [[Optic tract]]
    
- [[Lateral geniculate nucleus]] (in the [[Thalamus]])
    
- [[Visual cortex]] (located in the [[Occipital lobe]])
    

---

### 🧠 **[[Visual cortex]]**

The area of the brain where visual input is interpreted. Organized as:

- **Primary visual cortex (V1)** — raw feature detection (edges, contrast)
    
- **Secondary visual areas (V2–V5)** — motion, depth, object recognition, etc.
    

---

### 🧭 **Related Subsystems**

- [[Pupillary light reflex]]
    
- [[Saccades]] and [[smooth pursuit movements]]
    
- [[Binocular vision]] and [[depth perception]]
    
- [[Visual attention]] and [[pattern recognition]]
