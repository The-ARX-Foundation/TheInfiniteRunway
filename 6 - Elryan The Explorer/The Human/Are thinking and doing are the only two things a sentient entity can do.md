### **Networked Cores Model**

Instead of a strict table, you could visualize this as a **graph** with **Thinking, Doing, and Being** as nodes that interact dynamically.

- **[[Emotions]]** could be positioned at the intersection of **Thinking and Being**, as they contain both cognitive and experiential elements.
- **Creativity** sits between **Thinking and Doing**, as it requires ideation and execution.
- **Observation** bridges **Being and Thinking**, as it’s a state of passive awareness that informs cognition.

Could you define **"Being"** strictly in an experiential sense, or does it also include identity, self-perception, or existential presence?

Can _being_ be an active state, or is it inherently passive?

At first glance, _being_ seems like the absence of action or thought, a state of mere existence. But if we consider awareness, self-[[Reflection]], and presence as aspects of _being_, then it isn’t necessarily passive. Instead, _being_ might be a state that can be **cultivated** or **inhabited**, rather than simply defaulted into.

### **Active Being vs. Passive Being**

- **Passive Being** → Merely existing, letting experience flow without engagement.
- **Active Being** → Practicing presence, observing reality without judgment, embodying a state of awareness.

Consider mindfulness or meditation:

- You are **not thinking** in the usual analytical sense.
- You are **not doing** in the traditional sense of taking action.
- But you are still **aware, present, and experiencing**, which suggests an **active form of being**.

### **Can One "Just Be" Without Thinking or Doing?**

If _being_ were entirely passive, it would imply that it happens _to_ you rather than something you engage with. But the moment you consciously recognize and inhabit your existence, you're engaging in something different—an **active presence** rather than an unconscious state.

So, while _being_ often seems passive, you _can_ actively be. The paradox is that the moment you try to force it, you might slip into _thinking_ or _doing_. Instead, it’s about allowing yourself to inhabit the state without resistance—like floating rather than swimming.

But is _being_ something distinct, or is it simply a different _mode_ of thinking and doing?
