The main tenets of [[Creosticism]] are, as I see them
1. Creation is the ultimate good
2. Good should be strived for, so creation should be strived for
3. Destruction is the opposite of creation
4. Destruction is wrong, because it stops and undoes creation
There’s obviously more nuance to be had here, but that is about as simple as I can
get it without it becoming too simplified.
In this scenario, creation can be seen as anywhere on a spectrum from general to
hyper specific. A specific definition of creation would be “producing a piece of art,
media, idea, or thought with the express purpose of helping or being shared and
observed by at least one person”, which I believe would be a traditional reading of
creation as well. In this definition, art is creation, inventions are creation,
everything that people make for a purpose is creation. A more general definition of
creation would be “a byproduct of sentient existence that results from sentient
thought patterns, that can lead to more creation, rather than less.” This definition
is slightly circular, which is to differentiate it from destruction. Both creation and
destruction, in a general sense, are results from natural sentient existence; the
difference between them is that creation does not cut off options, but increases
them, whereas destruction decreases them. Creation is, in this sense, anything a
person does that positively affects them or others, and does not lead to an end of
choices or options. A person eating, taking care of themself, producing art, sharing
ideas, are all creation. Just by existing, a sentient being is both the ends and
means of creation, and by themself, is the creation that they are causing. Similarly,
by simply existing, destruction is also being caused by the sentient agent.
Destruction is simply that which removes future options, or something that has a
net negative. A person choosing not to take the creative path, rather to not take
care of themself, to [[Hurt]] others, to not produce art, is destruction. Additionally,
this is an inherent part of existence.
It is likely obvious that I much prefer one definition of creation over the other,
that being the general one. I think limiting what creation is would cause the wrong
focuses for individuals, and that creation as a concept is so far beyond a simple
understanding that the definition must also be vague. However, I believe that
creation and destruction are both inherent, opposites, and important. They are
opposites, yet they still cause each other. By choosing one creative option, an
individual is almost always choosing not to take another, which is in itself
destruction. If the future is an endless branching of pathways that an individual
could take, by choosing any one path, even if it is the creative path, the rest die
off, are destroyed by the ever marching tide of time. By choosing right now to
create this piece you are reading, I am not creating another work of art, or
bettering myself physically, or learning something new. Grieving this, however, is
futile. One cannot, and should not, take every creative option. One must choose
them based on what they desire, which must be guided by the overall maximum net
positive. This is not to say that one must take a utilitarian view on creative actions.
Because every action is simultaneously creative and destructive, there is no
objective best action, especially when the results are often obscured by the fog of
the future. Rather, one should take the action that they believe to be the most
creative, that maximizes their creativity in the present and future, and once they
have taken it, be sure in it as the right option. No one can see the future, so any
decision taken for creativity must be evaluated with the information known at the
time it was taken.
Creation must be strived for, and actions that create are good, however the
definition of creation must be wide to allow for all kinds of it. Additionally, we
must reconcile with the inherent destruction that comes with it. This destruction
is not in itself negative, but it can overwhelm the positives of creation. Destruction
is that which does not lead to creation, and which will lead to the end of options.
For an individual, destruction will lead to [[Death]], or the ultimate end of options. For
if an individual eating, taking care of themselves, and sleeping are of creation, then
not taking those actions is of destruction. Additionally, whenever an individual has
an opportunity to create but chooses not to they have in essence destroyed that
which they would have created. To put value to that which would have been created
is irrelevant, the morality of destruction, or lack thereof, does not come from
assigning value to the [[Death]] of some imaginary thing. Rather, the immorality of
destruction comes from the effect it has on the individual and others. If an
individual continually chooses destruction, they will die. If they choose destruction
more than creation, their natural creative tendencies, inherent to their being a
sentient agent, will frustrate them, leading to a mental [[Death]]. To justify this, I
want to use the modern education system as an example. Students are not told to
be truly creative, rather that is stifled in favor of more destructive activities. By
doing so, students end up with [[Frustration]] at the lack of creation, but do not
typically have the language to describe this [[Frustration]]. Creation can overburden a
person, but destruction will leave a person under fulfilled and unhappy, searching
for something they do not even know they are missing.
Creation and destruction both exist as parts of each other, and as parts of
existence. Both are important for continuing to exist as a sentient being. However,
creation creates options, whereas destruction removes them. In order to grow, a
sentient being needs more options, and to remove them. More options cannot have a
negative effect, whereas less options will have one. Because of this, creation is
morally good and should be strived for. Destruction will come, and is not something
to be strictly avoided, but a tool to use for more creations sake, so long as one
does not fall into its intoxicating grasp.
