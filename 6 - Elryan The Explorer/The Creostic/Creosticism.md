Themes: [[Philosophy]]

Blending [[Philosophy]], creativity, and spirituality into a framework centered on creation as a path to understanding, Creosticism aims to define a purpose for one to live; [[The Act of Creation|to create]]. 

The complications brought forth by most religion are shied away from; no holy place, no dogma, only [[The Act of Creation]] -- you can do it in your own home for all I care!

The following points lay the foundations for the framework:
### 1. **Philosophical & Theological Foundations**

- What are the core principles of Creosticism? 
	- The sanctity of creation, the value of [[Curiosity]], and the inevitability (and [[Acceptance]]) of change.
- How does Creosticism address existential questions (meaning, purpose, suffering, [[Death]]) through creation?
	- That is for the individual to decide; I personally believe that creation is a purpose in and of itself -- why else would we be able to do so in the first place? Meaning can be found by the [[Forming one's own belief]], the creation of ones own religion per-say. [[Death]] is inevitable, but suffering should be prevented through creation (see next point).
- Should Creosticism recognize "sacred" works of creation or view all acts of making as equally valuable?
	- The only sacred acts of creation are those uninhibited by external factors (greed, gluttony, etc.); creation to serve others without personal gain and creation to serve oneself for the purpose of growth are key examples of such.
- How does Creosticism define the relationship between the creator and their creation?
	- The creation is an extension of the creator, a way to be remembered and in a sense live on. The time a creator puts into their work is sacred in and of itself, as time is the most limited resource one possesses; the blood, sweat, and tears (a phrase of my time, not literal blood, sweat, and tears) put into an effort should directly contribute to the value. Further spiritual theory is left to the individual.

### 2. **Rituals & Practices**

- How can making and building be ritualized? (e.g., communal maker gatherings, solo creative reflections, annual celebrations of innovation)
- Should Creosticism have sacred spaces? (e.g., workshops, libraries, digital creation hubs)
	- I dont think it to be sacred, but I do want to start workshops, libraries, digital creation hubs, and archives devoted and available to those who practice. 
- How can Creostics share their creations as a form of philosophical dialogue?

### 3. **Community & Engagement**

- How do Creostics engage with each other? (Shared projects, collaborative problem-solving, philosophical discussions through creation)
- What ethical guidelines emerge from a creation-based spirituality?
- How do Creostics teach and pass down their wisdom? (Through artifacts, mentorship, open-source knowledge)

### 4. **Symbolism & Aesthetics**

- What symbols represent Creosticism? (E.g., a stylized hand, a forge, an unfinished sculpture)
- Should Creosticism have a sacred text, or is it an evolving, crowd-sourced codex?
- How can Creostics visually represent their faith through what they create?

### 5. **Historical & Comparative Analysis**

- Are there historical precedents for creation-centered spiritualities? (E.g., Gnosticism, Masonry, Hermeticism, Transcendentalism)
- What can be learned from Unitarian Universalism, Discordianism, and other non-traditional religious movements?
