### **1. Origins & Key Thinkers**

#### **Philosophical & Mystical Roots**

- **Plato (~400 BCE):** In _Phaedo_ and _Republic_, he describes the soul as pre-existing before birth and surviving after [[Death]], implying the body (including the brain) is merely an interface.
- **Vedantic Hinduism & Buddhism (~500 BCE–Present):** The mind is considered a conduit for _Atman_ (universal self) or _stream of consciousness_, rather than its source.
- **Rene Descartes (1596–1650):** Proposed _dualism_, suggesting mind and body are distinct, with the brain acting as an intermediary.
- **William James (1842–1910):** The father of American psychology, James speculated that consciousness could be independent of the brain and that the brain might act as a filter or reducing valve for a greater field of consciousness.
- **Henri Bergson (1859–1941):** A French philosopher who also proposed that the brain does not create consciousness but limits or transmits it, much like a radio receiver.

#### **Scientific & Parapsychological Perspectives**

- **Aldous Huxley (1894–1963):** Expanded on James and Bergson, proposing in _The Doors of Perception_ that the brain functions as a “reducing valve” for a greater, pre-existing consciousness.
- **Karl Pribram (1919–2015) & David Bohm (1917–1992):** Proposed the _holographic brain_ theory, suggesting the brain processes consciousness like a hologram, possibly tapping into a universal field.
- **Sir John Eccles (1903–1997):** Nobel-winning neuroscientist who believed consciousness exists outside the brain and interacts with it.
- **Roger Penrose & Stuart Hameroff (1990s–Present):** Their _Orch-OR theory_ suggests consciousness arises from quantum processes in the brain but leaves open the idea that it may be _non-local_—originating outside the brain.
- **Bernardo Kastrup (Contemporary):** Advocates _idealism_, arguing consciousness is fundamental to reality and the brain is merely a _modulation mechanism_.
