### 🛡️ The Wardings of a Public Obsidian Vault (Publishing with Intent & Security)

#### **1. Identity as Artifact, Not Access Point**

- **You are not your SSN.** Let your _public-facing identity_ be a mask of truth—accurate, but incomplete.
    
- Publish under a **moniker or mystic title** (e.g., _Elryan the Explorer_), and make it your _primary interface_. Your name becomes mythos, not metadata.
    
- In biographies or about pages, speak in _archetypes_ and roles. “I am a seeker, a builder of systems, a weaver of lost knowledge.”
    

#### **2. Operational Opacity**

- **Never** share:
    
    - Active, real-time location data.
        
    - Specific dates of travel, appointments, or movements.
        
    - Email addresses tied to government accounts, banking, etc.
        
- Time-delay personal content where possible. Let your past self be the _historian_, not the GPS.
    

#### **3. Cybersecurity Protocols**

- Host your vault via secure infrastructure:
    
    - **Static site generators** like [Quartz](https://github.com/jackyzha0/quartz), [Obsidian Publish](https://obsidian.md/publish), or [Hugo + GitHub Pages].
        
    - **Custom domain** + Cloudflare for DDoS protection.
        
- Use **privacy-first analytics** (e.g., Plausible, not Google).
    
- Sanitize frontmatter, internal links, and any `metadata.yaml` fields that may leak personal timestamps or editing data.
    

#### **4. Content Partitioning (The “Veil”)**

- Create **two vaults**, or layered views:
    
    - **Public Scroll**: Filtered, themed for others, curated.
        
    - **Private Grimoire**: Raw, encrypted, unfiltered.
        
- Write with **intentional omission**—not to deceive, but to protect.
    
- Use Obsidian plugins like _“Publish Later”_, _“File Hider”_, or tag-based selectors to filter out drafts or unready spells.
    

#### **5. Meta-Ward: The Index of Boundaries**

- Include a _Reader’s Oath_, a page that declares:
    
    - “This is a living document, shared with [[Trust]].”
        
    - “You enter as a guest, not a conqueror.”
        
    - And: “Here lies vulnerability—not invitation for harm.”
