"Mackenzie considers microorganisms as collectively complex forms, possessed of nonhuman sentience, allowing us to experience genetic modification in a relational context both within and beyond the laboratory."
The personification of the microorganisms we work with really brings into question how much we truly know about what we are dealing with. At the same time this insight might give way to a new form of understanding, a treatment of the microorganisms with the same respect we would a common houseplant; they may be what truly saves us in the end (as exemplified by the next quote). It seems as if their control stretches beyond what we consider today; the gut microbiome, for example, controls the vast majority of what we crave and conversely what we consume. What's to say there aren't similar forces at work among our other functions? What about other species (fungal control of ants)?
