## 📚 PewDiePie’s 2025 Reading List (with reasons)

### January – _Tao Te Ching_ (Laozi)

- **Why read:** Short, poetic, ancient Chinese wisdom. Accessible starting point. Focus on balance, [[Humility]], and living simply. Great “soft start” for rusty readers.

### February – _In the Buddha’s Words_ (Bhikkhu Bodhi, anthology of suttas)

- **Why read:** A direct gateway into Buddhist teachings. Offers a framework for self-examination, detachment from pleasure, and living mindfully. A transformative book for Pewds personally.

### March (Free Month – Pewds’ choice: Descartes’ _Meditations_)

- **Why read:** A pillar of modern [[Philosophy]]—questions existence, certainty, and the nature of the self. Bridges Eastern [[Introspection]] with Western rationalism.
    

👉 _Free month suggestion:_ Instead of Descartes, you could pick **Marcus Aurelius’ _Meditations_** (shorter, easier entry into Western [[Introspection]], also stoic, complements Buddhism).

### April – _Discourses and Selected Writings_ (Epictetus, esp. the _Enchiridion_)

- **Why read:** Stoicism distilled into practical life lessons. Strength through detachment, embracing hardship as training for virtue. A “handbook for life.”
    

### May – _The Republic_ (Plato)

- **Why read:** Sweeping discussion of justice, the soul, and the ideal society. Written as a dialogue—pulls you into philosophical reasoning. Ends with mythic imagery of afterlife and rebirth.
    

### June (Free Month – Pewds’ choice: Kant’s _Critique of Pure Reason_)

- **Why read:** Dense but foundational to modern [[Philosophy]]—on knowledge, perception, and morality. Very advanced; even Pewds frames it as a personal challenge.
    

👉 _Free month suggestion:_ Swap Kant for **Seneca’s _Letters from a Stoic_** or **Boethius’ _Consolation of Philosophy_** if you want something shorter but equally profound.

### July – _Nicomachean Ethics_ (Aristotle)

- **Why read:** Explores [[Happiness]] ([[Eudaimonia]]) as human flourishing, the role of habit, and the “Golden Mean.” Ancient but deeply practical—about becoming balanced and virtuous.
    

### August – _The Iliad_ (Homer)

- **Why read:** An epic classic. Explores war, honor, fate, and human mortality. The gods’ meddling adds mythic scale, but the ending strikes deeply with human emotion. Pewds’ personal favorite.
    

### September (Free Month – Pewds’ choice: Schopenhauer’s _The World as Will and Representation_)

- **Why read:** Proto-Buddhist Western philosopher. Pessimistic but powerful—life is will-driven suffering, but art and contemplation offer escape. A bridge to Nietzsche.
    

👉 _Free month suggestion:_ Instead, consider **The Bhagavad Gita** (India’s classic on duty, detachment, and self-realization—bridges East & West beautifully).

### October – _Thus Spoke Zarathustra_ (Nietzsche)

- **Why read:** Bold, poetic, misunderstood. Introduces “God is dead,” will to power, and Übermensch. Life-affirming [[Philosophy]] against nihilism. Wild, vivid, and challenging.
    

### November – _Siddhartha_ (Hermann Hesse)

- **Why read:** A fictional retelling of the Buddha’s life, blending Eastern and Western thought. About the journey to enlightenment through worldly experience. Accessible, moving, and beautiful.
    

### December (Free Month – Pewds’ choice: Hegel’s _Phenomenology of Spirit_)

- **Why read:** Complex, foundational for later philosophy. Explores consciousness, self, and history. Pewds admits he hasn’t read it yet—part of his personal “should do” list.
    

👉 _Free month suggestion:_ Instead of Hegel, try **Simone de Beauvoir’s _The Ethics of Ambiguity_** (modern existentialism, accessible, especially after Nietzsche).

## 🔑 Thematic Arc (Why this order works)

1. **Simple wisdom & self-mastery** (Tao, Buddha, Stoicism).
    
2. **Foundations of Western philosophy** (Plato, Descartes/Kant).
    
3. **Living well** (Aristotle, Stoicism revisited).
    
4. **Classics that shaped Western culture** (Iliad).
    
5. **Confronting suffering & meaning** (Schopenhauer, Nietzsche).
    
6. **Resolution through narrative & synthesis** (Siddhartha, Hegel).
