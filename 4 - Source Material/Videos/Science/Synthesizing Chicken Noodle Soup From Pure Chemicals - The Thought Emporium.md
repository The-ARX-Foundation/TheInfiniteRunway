## Food Science
### Introduction to Roast Chicken
Roast chicken is a common dish, available in most grocery stores. It's essentially the flesh of a dead bird, cooked to the point where the proteins denature, and the chemicals caramelize, resulting in a delicious, cooked-through meal.
### Composition of Chicken
Chicken is often seen as a base ingredient, but it's actually made of muscle, fat, bone, and connective tissue. These components are further made up of a list of chemicals, proteins, minerals, and metals.
### Taste Perception
When it comes to taste, not all the chemicals present in chicken are necessary for the flavor. Your sense of taste cannot discern every single ingredient, as many exist below the threshold of taste perception.
### Artificial Chicken Flavor
Artificial chicken flavor can be created by combining specific chemicals and a source of protein, then cooking the mixture. This process is based on a patent titled "Chicken Flavor and Process for Preparing the Same" (U.S. patent number 3689289), which provides a method for developing chicken flavor when heated in the presence of water.
### Taste Mechanism
The classic story of taste includes five types of taste receptors: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami (savory). These receptors are grouped into two categories:

- **Salty and Sour:** Detect changes in solution properties (salinity and pH).
- **Sweet, Bitter, and Umami:** Detect specific chemical groups.

Taste buds, which are embedded in papillae on the tongue, contain different cell types that detect these tastes. Type 3 cells detect sour and salty, Type 2 cells detect the rest, and Type 1 cells maintain the system. The brain combines taste signals and smell data to create the sensation of taste.
### Additional Taste Types

Beyond the classic five tastes, the tongue also detects oils, fats, and richness (kokumi), which come from short broken pieces of proteins found in cooked or fermented foods. These add depth and complexity to flavors.

### Protein Complexes and Olfactory Receptors

Taste cells express a heterogeneous population of taste and olfactory receptors. This means the tongue not only tastes but also "smells," contributing to a more detailed taste profile.

### Creating Fake Meat

To create an artificial meat flavor, you need to set up a reaction where heated components trick the brain into perceiving meat. This involves using biological matter that contains similar components to meat and fine-tuning the flavor with specific ingredients.

### Recipe for Artificial Chicken Flavor

The recipe involves blending ingredients and heating them to create the desired flavor. The key components include:

- **Soy Protein:** A bland protein base with a similar amino acid composition to meat.
- **Glucose (Dextrose):** Essential for the reaction generating meat flavors.
- **Defatted Soy Protein and Wheat Flour:** For texture and protein base.
- **Flax Oil:** As a fat source, similar in composition to animal fat.
- **Kombu (Arachidonic Acid):** Provides a meat-specific lipid found in brown algae.
- **Ribonucleotides:** Derived from hydrolyzed wheat or other sources, enhancing umami flavor.
- **Cysteine Hydrochloride:** A sulfur-containing amino acid crucial for meat flavor.

Additional ingredients that enhance the flavor include nutritional yeast and powdered dried mushrooms. Adjusting the levels of these ingredients can help mimic different meat flavors.

## Procedure

### Ingredients

- Defatted soy protein (textured vegetable protein)
- Glucose (dextrose)
- Wheat flour
- Flax oil
- Kombu (blended into a powder)
- Sauce containing ribonucleotides (hydrolyzed wheat, MSG)
- Cysteine hydrochloride (and possibly taurine, thiamine, methionine for variation)
- Nutritional yeast
- Powdered dried mushrooms

### Steps

1. **Prepare Soy Protein:** Blend defatted soy protein to de-texture it.
2. **Combine Ingredients:** Mix the de-textured soy protein, glucose, wheat flour, flax oil, powdered kombu, ribonucleotide sauce, cysteine hydrochloride, nutritional yeast, and powdered dried mushrooms.
3. **Blend:** Blend all ingredients together thoroughly.
4. **[[Heat]] the Mixture:** Cook the blended mixture in hot water or as part of another cooking process. The [[Heat]] will facilitate the reaction needed to develop the meat flavor.

### Notes

- **Salinity and Sweetness:** Adjust the salt levels to taste and ensure glucose is used for the sugar component.
- **Flavors:** Experiment with different combinations and levels of sulfur-containing amino acids to achieve the desired meat flavor. For richer flavors, increase the amount of mushrooms and kombu.
- **Final Adjustments:** For beef flavors, use taurine and thiamine, and add brown flavors like soy sauce, browning, miso, and balsamic vinegar.

By understanding and applying these principles, you can create a convincing artificial meat flavor that rivals real chicken or other meats.
