## Public Speaking CAN be learned.

Public speaking = information transfer
	IRL -> more engagement -> better information retention
		Less distractions

To make a good presentation:
	Define your "North Star"
		What is the key take away?
		Why are you talking to these people?
		Tell your audience what you are going to say
			"The point of my talk is _ "
		Say it
		Tell your audience what you said.
	Deliver your message to the target audience
		nobody gives a fuck about your widget
			Focus on the value it brings
		use common language (audience dependent)
			Jargon only if they are guaranteed to understand
	Do your homework
		KNOW what you're talking about
		"winging it" means that you dont memorize, but definitely write some scripts and Frankenstein em
	Persuasion:
		"Series of yeses"
			Yes I agree, oh that makes sense, thats why your product would be best!
		Don't instruct the customer
			Its insulting
			Assume the customer is the industry expert
			No:
				Why they're failing
				What they should do
			Yes:
				As you know ... (still tell them the thing tho)
		Know the particular customer
			What are their specific challenges?
				Frame around their needs
				Its not about you, its about the customers
			What level of nerd are they? 
				Tech vs Value
	Visual aids:
		If you use visual aids stand away from them so they can see lmao
			Keeps focus on you
		Your visual aid is a cue, you shouldn't read directly from them
	Be confident
		Speak up!
			Break the monotone
			Slow down if you are speaking too fast
				If you cannot be animated you're going too fast
		Make Eye contact
	Questions and Answers
		Think of the questions you want to be asked, maybe even leave some info out so there's a conversation
		Repeat the question and rephrase it if you can
			Lets the whole audience hear it vs. the whole audience
	Get feedback!!!
		"Never leave rejection empty handed, at least know why it went wrong"
	***==Plan a Follow up meeting==***
		"Let me get some more information for you"
		"Can we follow up next quarter?" (or whenever)
		Don't leave unless its scheduled if short term
			Send a calendar invite
	***==Thank the customer for their time.==***
		They're busy!
	They may try to hassle you
		Have thick skin, they're testing you
			Don't shut down, you'll never come back
	Practice!
		Role play

Interviews:
	Look into STAR Method
	Look into the 5 different interviewing styles

Sales Calls:
	Check in with existing customers
		Keep the relationship ongoing / active
		Get updated info
		No:
			"I'm just checking in" (unless thats all you're doing)
		Actually build the relationship; give them updates!
		Cold Call:
			"Is this a good time?"
			Otherwise you're wasting time; they're busy and wont be paying attention
		Face to face:
			Use calendar invites
				Don't give your customer tasks, YOU send the invite
				If can't decide just send a hard invite
					Time polls can be good, but be wary not to give them tasks
				If they suggest a time, take it and change your schedule around them.
	Ask specific questions (don't beat around the bush, theyre busy)
		"How's the product working for you?"
			"Can you tell me why its not working?"
			"What was missing?"
			"How could we do better?"
			You NEED to know the glaring flaws in your product.
			Negatives actually improve your relationship if you address them.
			Following up gives you opportunity to get more sales if its good!
		"Are you still planning on making a purchase?"
			Operating budgets & Capital Equipment budgets
				gonna be planned s.t. can factor in depreciation stuff
				Get their plans, when will funds be available to allocate to your widget
				Can be pushed forward or back; don't get caught surprised.
			"Is my company on the list to purchase from?"
				"What can we do to get on that list?"
				"What can we do to win your business?"

Customer categorization
	Unhappy
		Not going to be happy about new features
		"There's a patch coming for your issue"
	Conditionally Happy
		Don't experiment with them, they like it as is.
	Evangelical (sharing with others) / New adopters
		Would [[Love]] your new features

Investor Pitch: Focus on Return on investment.
	Hook:
		Set up the problem the customer is having
			Comparison between with and without product
				"Consistently 5x oil wells"
	Introduce yourself
	Expand on problem
		what made it difficult to solve
		what prevents someone else from solving it better?
	Explain your solution
		still series of yeses
		if you have data they'll think its lower risk
			working prototype >>>
	Introduce your team (shows their credibility)
	Ask
		what exactly do you want from the investors?
			How much money?
				Do some budgeting beforehand
				What are you raising funds for?
			Any perks?
			Don't necessarily bring in valuation immediately
				Reel them in, negotiate later
