# Manufacturing
Used to be all hand made
Moved to complex manufacturing with interchangeable parts
Complex manufacturing broken down into simple tasks
	Ford broke car manufacturing into 84 simpler tasks and brought the work to each worker
		Increased consistency
		Increased production rate
		Lowered cost of production
		Increased worker pay
Manufacturing became lean
	supply chain started leaning towards just in time manufacturing due to high cost of vertical integration
	by not ordering in bulk they could give customer more customizability
		inspired by supermarket
		"next years model" actually included current customer needs due to ability to change their manufacturing quickly
	Sub-assemblies created when needed
		errors could be detected and corrected so much quicker
			higher upfront, lower scrap cost
		determined by customer orders
	Ford was reluctant to switch over likely due to growing up in scarcity era (great depression) 
	 Revolutionary Japanese manufacturing practices
		"kanban" - precursor to barcoding
			Inventory traking for each part was mandatory to keep up with pace
		 "andon" - reduction of waste ASAP
			 Authority to stop production "pull the cord"
				Revolutionary, especially in japanese culture
			increased quality tremendously
		"kaizen" - continuous and incremental improvement
			even if small, they add up
		"jidoka" - human intelligence still in automation (i.e. the Andon cord)
	Waste elimination
		1. Large inventory
		2. Forecasting demand (overproduction)
		3. Continuous improvement
	Caveats with lean:
		Cost cutting with employees is bad
		Critical disruption risk if not properly diversified
		Methodology, not a standardized process
