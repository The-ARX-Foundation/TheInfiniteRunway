## 3. Do a little research on the global supply chain crisis of 2021-2023.  Briefly, give an example of a company that managed the crises well and one that did not.

The global supply chain crisis of 2021-2023 was driven by the COVID-19 pandemic, which severely disrupted labor availability and logistics. As demand surged post-lockdown, businesses faced shortages in raw materials, production delays, and transportation bottlenecks. Compounding this were geopolitical issues such as the Russia-Ukraine conflict, further straining supply chains.
### Example of Success: Walmart
Walmart managed the crisis relatively well by leveraging its massive distribution network and strong supplier relationships. The company increased its reliance on real-time data to manage inventory efficiently and adjusted its supply chain strategies by securing long-term [[Contracts]] with suppliers and increasing the use of chartered ships to avoid congested ports. Walmart’s investment in digital infrastructure and alternative transportation routes also helped minimize stockouts and meet customer demand during peak periods.
### Example of Struggle: Toyota
On the other hand, Toyota faced significant difficulties, particularly due to its reliance on "just-in-time" manufacturing, a strategy that emphasizes minimizing inventory to reduce costs. While this model is efficient under normal circumstances, it left Toyota vulnerable to component shortages, especially in semiconductor chips, a crucial part for modern vehicles. The company had to cut production across multiple plants, highlighting the risk of minimal inventory buffer during global disruptions. (They did end up recovering due to them being a very large company with cash to burn, but as stated the disruptions were not marginal).

Walmart's proactive adaptation and Toyota’s struggles reflect the broader challenges companies faced, showing how flexibility and long-term supplier relationships were key to weathering the crisis.
