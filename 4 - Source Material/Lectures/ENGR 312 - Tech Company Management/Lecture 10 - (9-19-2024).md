S&M - generates revenue
Operations - profitability

Efficiency / Effectiveness is a competitive edge
	less overhead = more profit
	ensure all resources and assets are utilized to their fullest potential
	reducing waste = more product
	coordination between departments = grease in the machine

Operations Types:
	Service
		Process to provide a service
	Retail & Distribution
		Inventory management and sales
	Manufacturing
		Process to produce a product
		Manufacturing:
			Transform raw materials into marketable products
				Repeatable outcomes
				Efficiency - speed
		[[Quality Control]]:
			Incoming raw materials
			Part inspection / rejection prior to placement in assembly
			Intermediary QC checks to minimize waste
			Finished product
		Supply Chain Management:
			Eliminate bottlenecks but keep cost down
			If there are issues upstream how do you mitigate this?
				"Plan for the unexpected" 
				Can purchase in advance, but then you have assets that aren't doing anything
					"Lean manufacturing"
					Sales fluctuations need to be accounted for
					Safety stock, dont necessarily push off in case shipping is disrupted.
				Can purchase at markup price; may be worth it.


Tolteq scale problem:
	How can you produce up to demand?
	How can you ensure products are good?
		Automation, having an engineer do this is too expensive


How to improve operations?
	Measure Key Performance Indicators (KPI)
		You cannot improve what you don't measure
		Dont measure for the sake of measuring, ask yourself what you get out of measuring this thing? Measure for the purpose of controlling and improving.
	Streamline
		Eliminate all redundancies and waste
		The process is never perfect, can always be improved
	Keep your eye on the industry
		If you don't improve you will be left behind by your competitors that will.
		Theres always new technology / techniques, find them and utilize them
			AI: Digital twins, visual inspections, route optimizations, Walmart procurement bidding bots
	Create a company mindset of continuous improvement

What can go wrong in operations?
	Focus on KPI that doesn't add much value
		Don't go for low hanging fruit, find the biggest areas of improvement
			Percentage of waste, "bang for buck"
	Lack of coordination among teams / departments
		Forecast for what isn't selling
			Sales knows when things will sell, [[Trust]] them.
			New products are scary for sales, they have their quotas
		Sales continue to sell products regardless of supply chain issues, 
			keep them updated on lead times so they don't disappoint the customers
		QC focus on wrong metric
			Ex: old machinist did finishing step that wasn't specified (de-burring chassis) 
		Operations not aligned with business strategy
			ex: moving to new market but still focused on old. 
				Cost vs. quality focused markets
