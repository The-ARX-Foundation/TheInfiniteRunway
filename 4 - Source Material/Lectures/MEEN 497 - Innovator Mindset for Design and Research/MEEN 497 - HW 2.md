### Prompt
- Please write an essay with your [[Reflection]] on the following:
    - What did you learn about yourself from the assessment?  Was anything surprising?  Did anything explain something you knew but didn't understand why?  Any new insights about yourself?
    - What do you plan to work on to improve your innovativeness?  Please discuss both the areas that you intend to work on and the actions that you will take to try to change your mindset and habits.

### Outline for Reflective Essay on Innovativeness Assessment

1. **Introduction**
    
    - Briefly introduce the concept of innovativeness and the context of the assessment.
    - Mention the purpose of the essay: to reflect on personal insights gained from the assessment and outline plans for self-improvement.
2. **Personal Insights from the Assessment**
    
    - **Discovery of Innovation-Focused Mindset**
        - [[Reflection]] on the realization of having an innovation-focused mindset.
        - Discuss how this mindset serves as a foundation for problem-solving and technological advancement.
    - **Surprising Revelations**
        - Highlight any unexpected insights, such as the understanding that mindsets are not fixed.
        - Discuss the significance of the relationship between increased innovativeness and value production.
    - **Clarification of Known Concepts**
        - Discuss how the assessment provided clarity on aspects of personal behavior, such as the [[Fear]] of failure and the influence of the status quo cycle.
        - Reflect on how these insights connect with past experiences in academia and research.
3. **Plans for Improving Innovativeness**
    
    - **Areas of Focus**
        - Identify specific areas where improvement is needed, such as risk-taking, flexibility in thinking, and the balance between knowledge and imagination.
    - **Actionable Steps**
        - **Mindset Shifts**
            - Discuss the importance of embracing failure as a learning tool.
            - Explore the need to challenge assumptions and separate facts from interpretations.
        - **Habit Development**
            - Outline specific habits to cultivate, such as treating ideas as questions, focusing on high-value outcomes, and seeking diverse perspectives.
            - Emphasize the role of imagination in generating new ideas and the importance of documenting them.
        - **Risk Mitigation**
            - Discuss strategies for managing risk, such as anticipating failure points and testing ideas virtually before implementing them.
            - Reflect on the need to make failure an acceptable outcome to encourage experimentation.
4. **Conclusion**
    
    - Summarize the key insights gained from the assessment and the importance of these reflections for personal and professional growth.
    - Reaffirm the commitment to continuous self-improvement and the development of a more innovative mindset.
    - End with a forward-looking statement on how these changes will enhance overall innovativeness.

## Landon Dahle - Initial IM Snapshot Reflection Essay

### Introduction:
As I navigate the fast-paced world of technology and innovation, I’ve realized just how important it is to understand my own mindset and how I approach creativity. Taking the Innovation Mindset Snapshot for this course gave me a chance to reflect on my thinking patterns, habits, and the way I tackle challenges, offering some surprising insights into how I solve problems and where I might need to adapt. This essay will reflect on the lessons learned from this assessment, explore the surprising revelations and clarifications it offered, and outline my plans for enhancing my innovativeness by adopting new mindsets and habits.
### Reflection
One of the most significant discoveries from the assessment was the confirmation that I hold a strongly innovation-focused mindset. This realization was both affirming and enlightening, as it highlighted how my outlook on technological acceleration serves as a platform for building ideals and crafting solutions. However, the assessment also brought to light that this mindset, while advantageous, is not static; it evolves with increasing exposure to innovative thinking and practices. I was surprised to realize how much my creativity and innovative thinking are directly tied to the tangible value I can produce, something I hadn’t considered in depth before. This insight left me curious about the metrics used to measure this increase and eager to explore ways to enhance my innovativeness further.
The assessment also clarified several concepts that I had previously recognized but hadn't fully understood. For instance, it highlighted the pervasiveness of the status quo cycle in academia and how it often stifles innovation by funneling individuals into predetermined roles. During my work in [[Biomedical Engineering]] research, I often found myself sticking to familiar methods I pulled from literature instead of experimenting with new ideas. This pattern, I now realize, reflects the status quo cycle that limits innovation. This cycle, coupled with an ingrained [[Fear]] of failure, has sometimes inhibited my learning and experimentation. Understanding these dynamics has provided me with a clearer perspective on why I sometimes hesitate to take risks or challenge established norms, particularly in environments where failure is not seen as an option.
After reflecting on the assessment, I’ve pinpointed a few key areas where I want to focus to boost my creativity. One of the biggest shifts I need to make is how I think about failure. For a long time, I’ve been afraid of failing, sometimes avoiding risks even when I knew they could lead to big breakthroughs. But now I see that failure is just part of the learning process. I’m learning to challenge my assumptions, step back from my initial interpretations, and stay open to new perspectives. It’s not easy, but I know that facing my [[Fear]] of failure head-on is going to help me tap into my full creative potential. I will reframe failure as a stepping stone, using it to test ideas and stretch beyond conventional boundaries.
Changing my mindset alone won't be enough to prevent the self-inhibiting behaviors I currently enact, I need to develop new habits that align with innovative thinking. One key habit will be treating my ideas as questions rather than definitive solutions. This approach will encourage continuous exploration and refinement of ideas, ultimately leading to more robust outcomes. Additionally, I will focus on high-value outcomes and seek diverse perspectives to ensure that my ideas are well-rounded and thoroughly considered. Engaging my imagination will also be a priority, as I aim to balance my knowledge with creative thinking. I plan to regularly document my ideas, allowing them to incubate and evolve over time.
Similarly, I need to re-think how I mitigate risk; I will work on managing risks in a way that allows for experimentation without the fear of catastrophic failure. This will involve anticipating potential failure points and testing ideas in a controlled environment before implementing them on a larger scale. By making failure an acceptable outcome, I [[Hope]] to foster a more experimental and innovative mindset.
### Conclusion:
Going through the assessment helped me see both my strengths and the areas where I can grow, which honestly was eye-opening. I hadn’t realized just how much my fear of failure was holding me back; now, by shifting my mindset, building new habits, and learning to take calculated risks, I’m committed to becoming more innovative and pushing my creative boundaries. I know these changes will help me grow both personally and professionally, allowing me to make a bigger impact in whatever I do. As I move forward, I feel excited about what’s possible and confident that these insights will be the foundation for a more fulfilling and innovative future.
