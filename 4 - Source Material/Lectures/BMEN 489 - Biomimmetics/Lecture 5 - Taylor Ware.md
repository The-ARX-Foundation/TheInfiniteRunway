## Reflection on Dr. Taylor Ware's Guest Lecture on Biomimicry

Dr. Taylor Ware’s guest lecture on biomimicry covered a variety of biological phenomena, from collective behavior in fire ants and black worms to the replication of the phenomena in liquid crystal elastomers and potential applications of hydrogels in a similar assembly for medicine. 
One of the most striking concepts presented was the idea that collective behavior often surpasses individual capabilities. The concept is widely applicable, in society, in academia, and of course as covered in the lecture engineering and medicine.

The provided examples of swarm robotics were exemplified to have potential applications in designing self-assembling materials and responsive biomaterials; the injectability of such seems a very convenient property that I [[Hope]] is explored further. Nature's efficiency in self-organization and resilience shines through when viewed from the objective lens done so in this lecture; a perspective I had yet to consider. With the [[Lectures]] focus on swarms and my own conversations about it, I was drawn to research the starfish this evening: their individual biology uses swarm-like tendencies with their feet (tube feet -- podia, often referred to as 'rays' apparently) utilizing collective action to move, their colonies move as one to travel at speeds unsustainable at the individual level (galloping almost), and even their embryos aggregate and move in chiral patterns (a 'living crystal'); a swarm animal at its peak. I do plan to do more research into them as such, and thought it relevant to include in this [[Reflection]] as it was directly inspired by the topics herein.

Reflecting on the usefulness of this lecture, I found it highly valuable in broadening my understanding of how interdisciplinary approaches—combining biology, materials science, and engineering—can lead to groundbreaking advancements. The insights into biomimicry, including the necessity of persistence and down-selection in research, were particularly motivating. Sentiments stated, "sometimes may feel aimless, but you have to accept it and keep at it! you'll find something worthy eventually" and "	bioinspiration is to find something to improve [[The Human]] condition" reinforced the idea that while bioinspired research might initially seem directionless, sustained effort can lead to meaningful contributions to human health and technology. This sentiment I feel can be applied generally in life; though things may feel aimless, with [[Trust]] and [[Hope]] in the process --with a little patience and persistence-- eventually good must come.

I would most definitely recommend inviting Dr. Taylor Ware back next semester, I quite thoroughly enjoyed his presentation and topics therein. I [[Hope]] to meet his acquaintance sometime later down the line to see how his research is going.
