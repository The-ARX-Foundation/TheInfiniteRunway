Life Summary:
* Tolteq, measurement while drilling (MWD) Kits; didn’t invent rather improved.
	* Bachelors EE, industry for 11 years, further experience in niche at Halliburton, moved to Austin but company got bought out.
		* Marketed skills via starting engineering contracting company

* Cold calls revealed not much luck; through persistence found a contract and built business on that specific contract.
	* Resourcefulness through making do with what he had. (Warehouse with no AC)

* Drive for engineering caused to keep going down path; starting development of their own MWD with another company.
	* Didn’t hire anyone on; “system worked part of the time”, customer lost confidence.
		* HIRE EXPERTS

* Looking for a source of improvement? Go to end-user!!!
	* Found there was a need for information on user end; there was no form of [[Troubleshooting]] up until Tolteq.
	* Found it should be retrofittable to old systems; provided path to market and testing ground.

* Made a full system but didn’t get sales.
	* “Perception Problem” – customers afraid to use it due to it being a new and unproven brand.
	* Put himself out there to do the job for free to advertise the product. (2 YEARS)
		* “Eating tools” – perfect opportunity to get insane data.
			* Huge sale - “Went from worrying about getting orders to worrying about filling orders.”
			* Shifted focus from designing products to designing a business and operations.

* 2016 – downturn in sales; not as much fun then, so decided to sell.
	* ITS OK TO SELL

* Entrepreneurship Tips:
	* Find a problem with a potential customer and fix it.
	* Finding customers:
		* “Pull the thread” – talking to people.
	* It’s a rollercoaster, and that’s ok!
	* Your education and access to people is the spark of your journey.
	* Pressure? That’s entrepreneurship.
		* Try to put people in place, but the final decisions are going to be yours.
	* Get a relationship with an attorney ASAP.

* Hiring:
	* **Self-starters**
		* Move them up as the company grew if they desire it.
		* Incentivize via bonuses / raises to keep employees.
	* Contracting / Recruiting agencies
		* Expensive but efficient
	* Think about a management structure earlier on.
		* 15-20 people max per management role.

* Partner Qualifications –
	* “Are they into it as much as you?”
	* Skillset? (Not as important but still good)
