* Let BIG-CO train you then go do your own business LOL.

* Start an LLC before marriage.
	*  Better to do it early in life
	* Regardless, you can fail all you need to.

* Never let people know you don’t know the answer unless you plan to find it.
	* Keep a good attitude.

* “Don’t let hierarchy take hold.”
	* A small desk with a crappy chair prevents people from complaining about theirs.

* “Poke the bear, you won’t beat him otherwise.”
	* Don’t be afraid to take on big competition, have faith in your product.

* Spend a LOT of time picking your founders; don’t pick your friends, you will become friends with your founders.

* Funds? – Friends and family if you can; you need payroll money not just starter money.
	* VC’s will take a BITE.
	* Write into [[Contracts]] clauses to make sure they can’t step in and take control as easily.

* VC needs:
	* Plan
		* Summary, timeline, supply chain, leaders, source or development of engineering?
	* Find a “Family VC”

* Get salespeople later, their relationship with the customer will dwindle over time.

* Leasing is ok, “conserve your plan while you’re going through the bumps”.
	* Learn to leverage your money.
	* Look into rent-to-own / compare interest rates to the leasing fees.

* Try to source things like HR, benefits, accounts payable, etc. from VC’s sister companies.
	* Can get off internet but tried and true is always better.
	* “Insperity” – HR company

* For supply chain; contract with variable rates / terms.
	* Start good for them, “for first $1M order: 50% on order, 50% once to Chinese dock”
	* Start moving towards you as orders go up![](file:///C:/Users/IOX20/AppData/Local/Temp/msohtmlclip1/01/clip_image002.jpg)

* The quickest and most efficient start is getting a product out then developing, though has caveat of reputation.
	* Sometimes can be better to develop and then launch with the best product.
	* If chosen, make the new thing cost more so it doesn’t obsolete your old equipment. “Purchase price variance” makes salespeople self-choose.

Pitch:
* Company summary
	* Who’s going to buy you? (Exit strategy)
* Each slide has corresponding pages.
* Target customers

Selling your company:
* Terms:
	* Initial Payment
	* “100 days to do full physical on inventory.”
	* Secondary Payment
	* “Bad debt collection”
	* Employer retention pool
		* 3.5 year stay agreement.

Misc:
* “You have to do all three”
	* Product
	* Service
		* Read “The New Gold Standard”
	* Reliability
