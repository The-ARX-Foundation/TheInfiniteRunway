* Intro by Seth Sullivan – 
	* “The best leader that I have ever gotten to be around.”
		* Personal [[Compassion]] from a CEO goes a long way

* “Don’t shy away from hard work.”
	* Engineering was the right choice.

* “Graduate school opened up my world.”
	* “Expose yourself to as much as you can.”
		* Get some industry experience then go back to grad school once you have found your niche.
		* Only caveat was that it missed the [[Connection]] to people.

* The best people are those that “believe that they can make it work”, regardless of if they know how they will.

* “If you’re going to lead anything, you must first lead yourself.”
	* Spend time reflecting on yourself, your situation, what you are here to do.

* “It takes a lot of work to have partners.”
	* Find a clear idea of what you want to accomplish and stick with it; bringing on a partner makes it easier in the short run but if there are ANY deviations then it’s going to be tough.
		* If you aren’t aligned, you must address that; have a conversation and determine what must be done. 

* It will feel like a “war of attrition” and that “the world is against you” because it is.
	* The job is finding a way to get through all of that.

* Two valid paths in owning a business, owning and maintaining or selling.
	* Selling will make more up front, but it is selling a piece of yourself.
		* To “maintain the momentum”, you must have the drive to maintain it yourself if you think you can expect someone else to do the same.

* Think about your business as an “organic thing”; it must be fed and molded to prosper. “You must give it its essential nutrients and prune it when necessary.”

* “What do YOU want out of it.”
	* Only pursue your venture if you yourself want it.

* Bringing family into the business was a designed process for Zachary Group.

* Just like having kids, having a business is a whole lot more than what you expect.

* “Its not just hard work while you’re working, you’re essentially dedicating your whole life.”
	* It’s worth it.

* “Strategic impact with employees”: 2 vs 20k employees, it’s the same thought process.
	* How they feel about their circumstances completely impacts the way that they work, and thus how your product turns out.
	* Build your business around supporting your employees so that they can support the customer.
	* You’re not hiring them just to do a job, you’re hiring them for their humanity, their personality.
		* When people are free to offer more than just their work they will. Engagement goes a long way.

* How can you hire entrepreneurs? People with creative thinking?
	* Dedicate capacity within the organization that focuses on strategizing / growth / new developments. 
	* “Encourage people to offer their ideas, and believe that those ideas are appreciated, respected, and valued even if not utilized.”
		* Must find a way to keep people engaged even if the path chosen is against their idea.

* “That is the art of leadership.”

* Staying private allows for LONG term investments. It allows for the freedom to make the decisions YOU want to do. 

* What do you look for in purchasing a company?
	* Does the business do something that you don’t do that you must acquire?
		* If you can move to do it yourself that would be best.
	* Talent to bring into the organization. 
	* Cultural compatibility with your current company culture.
