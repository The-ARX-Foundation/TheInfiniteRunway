The article titled "The Market Size Dilemma: Fixing TAM, SAM, SOM and Telling the Story of Your Market" by Raymond Luk provides insight into the often frustrating process of estimating market size and framing it in a pitch deck. Let's break down the key points and their significance:

### Outline

1. **Introduction to Market Sizing Challenges**
    
    - Entrepreneurs often lack rigor when calculating market size.
    - Investors expect precision, but market size is inherently uncertain.
    - The real focus should be on assumptions behind the market size.
2. **Pre-requisites for Calculating Market Size**
    
    - **Market Segmentation**: Broad categories like "small businesses" need to be broken down into specific sectors (e.g., restaurants, accountants).
    - **Revenue Model & Pricing**: Both will likely change, impacting market size.
    - **Estimating Number of Customers**: Requires educated guesses rather than exact data.
3. **Assumptions Behind Market Size**
    
    - **Customer Numbers**: Assumptions about market share, future growth, and competitive encroachment.
    - **Customer Segments**: Will you stay within your initial target or expand?
    - **Revenue Model**: Factors like pricing changes, churn rates, and ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) must be considered.
4. **TAM, SAM, and SOM - Simplified**
    
    - **TAM (Total Addressable Market)**: All money spent on solving a problem.
    - **SAM (Serviceable Available Market)**: The target market you're addressing.
    - **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**: The revenue you expect to achieve, usually mid-term (e.g., 5 years).
    - Use these acronyms to go from high-level market vision to the specifics of your target and obtainable market share.
5. **How to Really Look at TAM**
    
    - Traditional TAM, SAM, and SOM visuals (three circles) are misleading as they don’t account for competitors and alternatives.
    - Consider dynamic market forces: shrinking markets (e.g., CD-ROMs) and emerging competitors.
    - Think beyond static numbers: Is the market growing? How do competitors influence your potential?
6. **Redefining the Addressable Market**
    
    - Market definition can be too narrow. Founders must sometimes rethink their TAM, especially for disruptive products.
    - **Example: Uber**: Reframing the market from “taxis and limos” to “all transportation” massively expands the TAM.
    - **Example: Peloton**: Framing Peloton's market as the intersection of exercise, gaming, and spinning broadens the scope.
7. **Summary - Telling the Market Size Story**
    
    - Focus on a bottom-up approach and tell a compelling story.
    - The story should prompt investors to think about larger possibilities (e.g., "but couldn’t it be even bigger?").

---

### Key Takeaways

1. **Market Sizing is Complex**: It’s not just about finding a number, but about the assumptions behind it. Founders must segment the market, create a preliminary revenue model, and make educated guesses on customer numbers.
    
2. **Assumptions Matter**: Founders need to be transparent about their assumptions regarding customer growth, revenue models, and competitive factors.
    
3. **TAM, SAM, and SOM Need Context**: While these acronyms are standard, they require careful thought and a narrative that can shift based on market conditions and competitor actions.
    
4. **Reframing the Market is Powerful**: Disruptive products may redefine existing markets or create entirely new ones. Founders should think broadly about what markets they could disrupt.
    
5. **Tell a Growth-Oriented Story**: Investors want to see the potential for growth, even if your initial market size doesn’t seem large. Invite them to imagine the future where your market could expand.
