## Consumer Market
### **TAM (Total Addressable Market):**

According to a survey conducted in 2021, around 89 percent of respondents owned at least one plant in 2020. [1] With 127 million (2022) households in the US [2] this can be extrapolated to total number of houseplants in the US by

$\text{Rough Estimate of US Houseplants} = 127 \, \text{Million Homes} \times 0.89 = 113 \, \text{Million Houseplants}$

Estimating a cost per widget of $10 this translates to 

$\text{TAM} = 113 \, \text{Million Plants} \times 10 \, \text{USD} = 1.13 \, \text{Billion USD}$

### **SAM (Serviceable Available Market):**

To estimate **SAM**, we need to narrow down the target to a specific market segment. In this case, targeting **people who want to automate** plant care and management. To refine the SAM, let's assume that automation or advanced technology solutions appeal to a smaller subset of the total market.

A plausible estimate could be that **77% of the total U.S. indoor plant market** would be interested in adopting automation solutions (based on a survey of the Top 100 Growers [3] ) for reducing plant mortality, particularly businesses or advanced hobbyists.

$\text{SAM} = 1.13 \, \text{Billion USD} \times 0.77 = 0.87 \, \text{Billion USD}$

Thus, the **SAM**—the portion of the U.S. indoor plant market that might be interested in an automated solution—could be around **$870 Million**.

$\text{SOM} = 0.87 \, \text{Billion USD} \times 0.20 = 0.174 \, \text{Billion USD}$

## Supplier Market
### **TAM (Total Addressable Market):**

We know that the US plant market in 2021 was **$2.17 billion** [1], but due to the **40% plant mortality rate** [2] in the supply chain, this only accounts for 60% of the total potential market.

To calculate the total U.S. indoor plant market (including the plants that are lost in the supply chain):

$\text{Total U.S. Market} = \frac{2.17 \, \text{Billion USD}}{0.60} = 3.62 \, \text{Billion USD}$

This implies that the **total U.S. market** could be **$3.62 billion** if all plants survived the supply chain. 

If the solution targets the 40% of plants that die, the obtainable portion of the U.S. market would be:

$\text{TAM} = 3.62 \, \text{Billion USD} \times 0.40 = 1.45 \, \text{Billion USD}$

Thus, the **TAM** (the portion of the U.S. market addressing supply chain mortality) would be around **$1.45 billion**.

### **SAM (Serviceable Available Market):**

To estimate **SAM**, we need to narrow down the target to a specific market segment. In this case, targeting **people who want to automate** plant care and management. To refine the SAM, let's assume that automation or advanced technology solutions appeal to a smaller subset of the total market.

A plausible estimate could be that **77% of the total U.S. indoor plant market** would be interested in adopting automation solutions (based on a survey of the Top 100 Growers [3] ) for reducing plant mortality, particularly businesses or advanced hobbyists.

$\text{SAM} = 1.45 \, \text{Billion USD} \times 0.77 = 1.12 \, \text{Billion USD}$

Thus, the **SAM**—the portion of the U.S. indoor plant supplier market that might be interested in an automated solution—could be around **$1.12 Billion**.

### **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):**
Further narrowing down to specific pricing strategies, if we were to target suppliers we could estimate the number of sales and apply a prospective price to see what amount we would make.
