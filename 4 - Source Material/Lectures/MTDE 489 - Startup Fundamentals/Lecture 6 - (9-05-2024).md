## Q/A with Prof. Deere
Don't write notes during the convo
	"That was really good point, would you mind if I write it down?"
You can hide your method by keeping it high level.
Mirror their body language and formality level
	Try to be more open though
"Does this sound exciting to you?"
	Fish for what they like and build on it
Ask about a problem you know you have an answer to to intro your solution.
If you dont know:
	"We're doing discovery, but it sounds like you're interested!" 
	"What would most interest you?" 
	"Would you want to pilot our solution?"
Conclusions:
	Make a follow up

## Presentation HW: 
1.  What did you learn from your customer discovery presentation?
DONT MEMORIZE THINGS :/. Dan and I did practice a lot, but I had some residual bits memorized that when I didn't perform on I froze up (also thought itd be presentation to class mainly, so wasn't prepared for one on one conversational style and I got in my own head that I sounded preachy.)
2.  List, in order, your 3 favorite presentations.
Soil Sensors, Solar Panels, Fire/[[Heat]] Resistant Coatings
3.  What made an elevator pitch good?
In this setting the goal was to almost be conversational, so organic flow and talk-back was key to get customer engagement. Asking questions helped a lot. Re-integrating key points from the conversation showed you're listening to the customer rather than just word-vomiting.
4.  What made an elevator pitch not-so-good?
Freezing up, talking over the customer, talking over your teammate, talking too fast, preachiness, manipulating the customer (one felt like gun under table at times).
