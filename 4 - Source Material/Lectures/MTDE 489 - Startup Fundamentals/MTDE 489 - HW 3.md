1.  ***What is the main difference between between Seed Funding and Series A funding?***
	Seed funding helps the startup get off the ground with small investments and a focus on early validation; contrarily, series A funding is for scaling, with larger investments aimed at accelerating growth after early success is proven. 
2.  ***Think about the funding process discussed in class and included in the slides notes, is there any thing about the process that you found surprising?  If you ran a startup would you or would you not seek outside funding?***
	One thing that most surprised me is the lack of utilization of crowdfunding for larger companies; I feel that it may become a larger source in the future as individual ownership and trends such as blockchain come into play. I am a big subscriber to the bootstrapping mentality until it is absolutely necessary to get funding for scaling. The analogy of owning the entirety of a small pie vs a slice of a larger pie aptly sums up the thought process for giving up equity for a funding launchpad.
3.  ***What is your big takeaway from Case Study #1 and our class discussion.***  
	Though not as big of a point in the class discussion, one of my biggest takeaways from the case study is the need to define the goals you have for the company and ensure everyone is on the same page; the two founders VV Tikekar and Reza Rastegar were not necessarily on the same page and it seemed to be a point of contention. Additionally, figuring out potential revenue streams is a need if you're getting funding outside of personal connections or the unicorn that is an investor that wants to invest in [[The Vision]]/goal being achieved rather than a definitive ROI. 
4.  ***What is your big takeaway from Case Study #2 and our class discussion.***
	An unethical business practice likely won't be sustainable. In Theranos's case bad leadership combined with flawed processes, lack of transparency, and unchecked overconfidence led to its downfall, highlighting the dangers of neglecting proper scientific and ethical practices in the medical field.
5.  ***What is your big takeaway from guest speaker Andrew Kazlow?  What could Mr. Kazlow do to improve his talk?  Would you recommend him as a speaker for this class next semester?***
	Mr. Kazlow's talk was genuinely super informative into the world of angel investors; his business's "Angel Ops as a Service" approach highlighted the needs of the community and how he was able to find his fit serving those needs. My biggest takeaway from his talk was not from the viewpoint of an investor, rather from someone who may be in the chair asking for funding; specifically points about what angels look at (that being the market, team, financials/ROI, traction, and how de-risked the business is) and why startups fail (that being one of the following: founder quits, they run out of cash, they lack of market fit, poor execution, co-founder conflict, pivot too late or too many pivots). I would definitely recommend him as a speaker for next semester and wouldn't change much about his talk other than potentially finding quicker ways to engage the audience other than asking for questions (was a slow build-up as we got acquainted).
