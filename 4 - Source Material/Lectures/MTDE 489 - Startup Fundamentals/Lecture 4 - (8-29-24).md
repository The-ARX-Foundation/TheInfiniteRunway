## Legalese
### Why to incorporate a business
Incorporating your business: (you need a solution to a problem that someone will pay for first)
	1. Asset Protection
		Separates you personally from the business. (unless extremely egregious or you "break the corporate veil")
	2. Creates a Corporate Identity
		Inc. or LLC at end of your name shows legitimacy
	3. Perpetual Life of the Business
	4. Ability to Transfer Ownership
		Exit!
	5. Ability to Build Credit and Raise Capital
	6. Some Tax advantages
		401K, Health and Life Insurance paid with pre-tax funds

### Types of Corps:
1. Sole Proprietorship
	Advantages
		No Cost
		Simple DBA Document
	Disadvantages
		No asset protection
		Unincorporated Business
		Taxes as Personal Income
			Though can deduct expenses from personal income
2. LLC (Limited Liability Company)
	Advantages
		Low Cost
		Profits flow through to your personal taxes; distributed to owners
		Owners are not personally liable for company's debts or legal obligations
	Disadvantages
		You still have to pay self-employment taxes (FICA: medicare & social security)
		Only one class of stock
			All have equal rights regardless of contribution
				Can distribute percentages, though rights remain the same
					Pulling money out = paying everyone
				Voting still based on percentages though
3. S Corporation
	Advantages
		Owners can give themselves a salary to limit the amount of self-employment tax
			Regardless of percentages
			Entity still pays the tax, but it is paid by entity
			Investments are paid post-tax
				Purchasing an asset is an investment not an expense
	Disadvantages
		Limited to only 100 shareholders
		Only one class of stock
		Must be US citizen or resident
4. C Corporations (Delaware has advantages)
	Advantages
		Completely separate entity
			Corporation is taxed at separate rate
			Multiple classes of stock
				Conditional ownership
		Most VC require C-Corp s.t. they can have their preferred stock
			They get paid first, like a loan.
	Disadvantages
		Owners are double taxed, once at corporate rate and again on personal income
			only on distributions
		Losses stay within the company - not disbursed to owners taxes
		Subject to SEC rules and regulations
		Must have board of directors

### Creating a corporation
What happens when you create a corp?
	KO by K1 - Tax burden too much to afford ownership
		Make clause to give as much distribution to pay tax
	1. Federal Taxes
		Creates EIN
			Files taxes annually based on EIN
			Bank accounts 
	2. State Taxes (based on inc. state)
		Texas doesn't have state income tax on citizens
			Franchise Tax - on revenue
				Applies Taxes to top 70% of revenue
				Revenue less COGS - Gross Revenue
				Revenue less compensation
				Revenue minus $1 million
			Tax Rate Brackets
				<$1,230,000 - No Tax
				0.375% if retail (already paying sales tax)
				0.75% if not retail
				0.33% if taxable amount is > $20 million
			Sales Tax 
				Collect on sale to end user
				Report and pay timing dependent on the amount collected
				Tax rate is dependent upon location, varies between 6.25% and 8.25% (added by counties and municipalities)
	HIRE EXPERTS, ITS WORTH THE MONEY
		Attorney for incorporation
			Can be amended; dont necessarily have to re-incorporate
		Accountant for filing taxes
			THEY DONT DO YOUR BOOKS
			Keep em clean
				Know where money comes from and goes
				Expense vs Asset Purchase
				Clean P&L Statement and Balance Sheet
				Review Weekly
		Insurance (Company Liability Insurance)
			As soon as you start to go to consumer you are at risk of lawsuits

Typical Path:
	Typically start as LLC
		DO NOT HAND OUT EQUITY AS AN LLC
		"Keep your cap table clean; VC's will not even consider you if not"
			Former owners can take legal action against the company
				Happens when company makes money or receives large investment
	Convert to S-Corp or C-Corp 
		S-Corp if making bank
		C-Corp 
			if depending on investors
			if want to distribute shares to employees

READ YOUR DOCUMENTS
	"Don't matter" (until theres a problem)
	If you can have an attorney look at em early it makes things better down the line
		If you think there will be a disagreement, clean up your documents before addressing the issue.
	How to add/remove owners
	Voting Rights
		Majority vs. Super Majority
			Dont want small equity owner blowing up plans
	Dont want people selling their equity to people you don't know

Company Dissolution:
	All assets are liquidated
	Payouts:
		Employees > Vendors > Loans (Secured by Collateral) > Shareholders
								VCS want to be here   ^

### IP Protection
Patents:
	Types:
		Design
		Utility
		Plant (like actual plants lmao)
	Can get provisional patent
		12 months, placeholder patent
		At date of provisional patent
	Terms:
		If you disclose your unique idea to the general public you get a monopoly on capitalizing on that idea for 20 years
		Only gives you right to sue violators on your patent
			ONLY WORTH IT IF YOU CAN PROVE IT
			Good if you're trying to exit (bigger company can and wants to enforce)
PCT Filing - Cross-Country protection
Copyright:
	Covers authorship
	Terms: Life of author + 75 years
		Becomes public domain after
Trademark:
	Branding
		10 Years + unlimited renewals
