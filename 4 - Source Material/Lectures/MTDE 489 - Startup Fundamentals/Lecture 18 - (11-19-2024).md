# Exit Strategies
## Determine your companies value
Three main categories:
### Early Stage
"Funding Method"
Depends on what you and the investor can agree upon for a price
### Mid Stage
Product market fit defined, making revenue
"Revenue Method"
Revenue for next 12 months times multiplier
	Multiplier based on agreement, but range calculated by:
		Growth rate 
		Purchase Recurrence

### Late Stage
Mature company
"Price to Earnings Method" 
Profits x P/E ratio
	P/E ratio look up for similar company that is publicly traded

## Determine the type of exit
Successes:
### IPO

### Acquisition
### Legacy

Failures:
### Liquidation

### Bankruptcy
If incorporated

## Acquisitions (Deere focus due to experience)
### "Companies are bought, not sold"
Entice people to come to you with an offer
Only way to get a good deal

### How do you get your name out there?
Depends on purchaser, but typically: awards, press, etc.

### Usually Purchased by:
Development Partner
Key Supplier
Distribution Partner
investor trying to do a roll-up
Rarely someone you don't know 
Another company in the same industry
	Strategic Threat Acquisition
	Revenue generation
		Vertical integration
		Horizontal Acquisition (similar products)
		Market Extension Acquisition
			Moving into different regional areas
		Conglomerate Acquisition
	Acqui-Hire
		Bring you along with company (likely not what you're looking for)
		Typically type of fire-sell

### Investor Relations
Not always on same page between investors
VC's typically require 3x
	Clauses to stop any sale
The more investment money you take the harder it is to exit
	Take as little as you can
	Think about potential buyers at 3x the investment pull

### Deere's Story
The company he purchased key sensors from (NOV) started getting in the door
He didn't know they wanted an acquisition until they started asking financial questions
Note: GET AND NDA
Later on in the process you will get a Letter of Intent (LOI) that they want to purchase you or something within your company
	Don't sign, let the date expire
	The price only goes down from what you sign
	Have your team look into EVERYTHING
		Keep everything you have ever signed to ensure no clauses come into play if you sell
	Negotiate
		Price
			Delivery Method
				If large company, opt out of earn out in the case their goals change
					"pay me enough that I don't have to [[Worry]] about the rest"
				Cash vs. Stock
					Set boundaries, don't negotiate if you wont accept anything
		Hold-backs
			Quantity & Time
		Indemnifications
			i.e. not holding you responsible
			Veto this, you don't want the liability to be immortal
Deere went through 6-7 LOI's
Eventually got to the point he was happy and signed. This is a legally binding contract

After signing they perform their own due diligence; looking for reasons to discount the price
	Better your records, the better this goes for you
	A mountain of documents
	Takes 6 weeks - 6 months
	Keep this from your employees and vendors in the case that it goes south; people get nervous

Purchaser writes [[Contracts]]
	They want to put things in place for them
		Employment contract for founders
		Non-compete
		Working capital
			Hard sell
			(how much money you leave in the company)
	Pay your attorney hella to go through this

#### Advice
Gather the people required for this well in advance
	People you can [[Trust]]
	Lawyers
	Accountants
	Board relationships
	Investors as advisors

Understand from the M&A team, this is just another day in the office
	They're just trying to get the best deal they can for the company
	They're not helping you out
	They're not your friend

Think, if the company can do better under someone elses rule, let them take over!!
	Not to say you can't make it insane
	Just take solace in taking an offer
	Think of your employees
	If you are [[tired]] of things, let it go.
