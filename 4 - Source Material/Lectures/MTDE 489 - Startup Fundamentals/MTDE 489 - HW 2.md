1.  What is Lean Launch and what is it used for?
	Lean Launch refers to the methodology grounded in the principles of Lean Startup, which emphasizes customer discovery, rapid experimentation, iterative learning, and quickly critiquing ideas. It’s often used by entrepreneurs to validate their business ideas early on with minimal resources; instead of building a full product, entrepreneurs use prototypes or minimum viable products (MVPs) to test hypotheses about market needs and customer preferences, refining their approach based on real-world feedback. The goal is to reduce waste and maximize learning about what customers truly want before fully investing in product development.

4.  List and explain the 3 types of patents.
	Utility Patent: This protects the functional aspects of inventions, such as machines, processes, or manufactured products. It covers how something works, is made, or is used.
	Design Patent: This protects the ornamental design of a functional item. It covers how a product looks rather than how it works.
	Plant Patent: Granted to anyone who invents or discovers and asexually reproduces a new and distinct variety of plant, this patent protects new plant varieties that can be replicated without seeds.
5.  List and explain the 4 types of IP.
	Patents: Protect inventions and grant the inventor exclusive rights to make, use, and sell the invention for a set period.
	Trademarks: Protect words, symbols, logos, or designs that distinguish goods or services from others, safeguarding brand identity.
	Copyrights: Protect original works of authorship, such as books, music, and software, giving creators exclusive rights to use and distribute their works.
	Trade Secrets: Protect confidential business information, such as formulas, practices, or designs, that give a company a competitive edge (e.g., the Coca-Cola recipe).

	The most important part of a non-provisional patent is the claims section, where the claims define the legal boundaries of the invention's protection, specifying what is patented and what others are prohibited from using without permission. This section, often written in precise and technical language, dictates the scope of the patent and determines how enforceable it is in legal contexts.
