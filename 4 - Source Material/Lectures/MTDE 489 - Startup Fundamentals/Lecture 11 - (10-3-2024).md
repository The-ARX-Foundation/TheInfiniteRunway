(Lecture 8 - Sales)

At this point you should have:
	Great Idea
	Customer Discovery
	Proof of Concept
	Incorporation
	Prototype & Customer Feedback
	Funding

Transition from ideation to growth phases
	"When should I start selling?"
		The day you become a business
		If you don't make sales then your business will never make it
	The best way to fund your startup is selling something to your customer!
		Bootstrapping
			You're short staffed and cash strapped
			You need to be the original sales team
				You are the most passionate about the product
				You are the one who will make changes to the product, so skip the communication gap and go straight to the customers.
		Even if its not enough; having sales opens funding opportunities for less equity
	You're going to have to figure this out on your own if you're in a startup.
		Big-co = sales infrastructure
			Defined Markets
			Leads to pursue
			CRM to work with
			Sales process
			Sales personnel to sell for you
		Often need to define the market yourself
			Find those leads
			"Set the stage" for your sales people; if you don't they will fail no matter how good they are.
				Have expectations
				"Define the sales infrastructure and process"
	Someone on the team needs to be the customer advocate
		listen to the customers objections
		No matter the cost, the customer needs must come first in order to actually succeed
	Number 1 Tip:
		Sell the problem that you are solving.
		Avoid:
			Leading with feature dumping your product
				 Can do once hooked if they're into it but dont lead with it
			Discussions about you, your startup, how great you are at being an engineer/entrepreneur, etc.
				Its about the customer and their problem that you're solving
	New ideas are slow to catch on:
		People have solutions! You need to figure out how to get them to switch
		In reality, most products are not that new "me too product with a twist"
			Built in market (who is the competitions market?)
			Approach sales by saying why yours is better (embrace the "me too")
				"Preface Problem Feature Benefit"

Market Definition:
	Identify target market
	Identify end user and customer
	Plan how you approach the market
		Type of sales?
		Sales channel?

How to get started:
	Sell to the following personas in a company:
		The Sourcer - Interested in utilizing your product
		The Decision Maker - Who is the final decision maker? (typically higher up manager / owner)
		The Utilizer - Who implements your product?
		The End User - Who uses or benefits from your product?
	EX: Tolteq
		The Sourcer - MWD Manager
		The Decision Maker - The owner (VP of MWD Operations)
		The Utilizer - MWD Field Personnel (Ease of implementation turns them into the sales guy on the inside)
		The End User - Oil company drilling a well (Raise their bars and you become their first choice)
	Ex2: Selling to distribution company
		The Sourcer - Whoever finds products for the company
		The Decision Maker - purchaser or VP of distribution company
		The Utilizer - retail outlet
		The End User - purchaser of product from retailer
	For each persona decide:
		What is their motivation for choosing your product?
			Why do they see value in it?
	Frame a pitch to each persona
		Don't get stuck on one pitch
		You want the customer to talk and interrupt you, answering their questions lets them sell themselves the product.
			Watch how your audience responds and make the adjustments as needed
			(Scientific method / lean launch)
	Role play to get feedback on how your pitch sounds to others
		Have them put up objections so you
	Go out and sell it!!

Selling day one:
	No product? Get some leads
		Become an expert in the field (you need to be active in the industry)
			Who sells what to whom?
			Why do people buy product A over product B?
		Build a sales framework (make some money lol)
			Sell your time as an expert - consulting
			Sell data you have gathered
			Sell a hybrid of data and a low level product
				Ex: starting a repair business, produces product that revolutionizes industry
					test parts piecewise, use the other products to figure out what works
						Tolteq using repairs to test board types
				Ex2: Distribution personnel poaching market lol
			Sell [[The Vision]] of your startup
				"Wouldn't it be great if the industry could do this?"
		Build and manage your sales pipeline
			Qualify your customers:
				Who is really interested
				Do they have the money/budget to buy?
					Don't waste time on people that don't if you aren't getting anything else out of it
						Have them influence others to buy it
						Customer feedback
					Dont waste time on deals that won't close or bring value
					Don't think if they accept something free they'll purchase the product
						Charge a nominal fee if you can, if they aren't willing to pay then they most definitely pay full price.
					Don't negotiate price, focus on features.
				Identify early adopters
				Find people who are risk averse and keep tabs on them
					Once you get some sales you can go back, they already have the seed of the idea in their heads.
			Build your CRM
				What are you going to hand off to your first salespeople
		Closing the deal:
			Be confident in your offering and the value it brings
			Don't get offended if the customer gets negative about your product
				Address the points if they are worth addressing, but keep your head up (your product has value!)
				What else can you do to make the sale other than price?
					Find the objections
					Sometimes even just a smokescreen
			Keep asking what needs to be done to close the deal.
				Never leave a meeting without new information
				Never take rejection empty handed (understand the why)
			Its ok to be upfront
				"What do I have to do to make you become a customer today?"
					You will learn a lot from the answer
						If not your product: Legal, budgeting, approvals, etc.
							Come back later!
							"Would it be helpful if I were to reach out to _ (higher up)?"

How do you manage everything?
	Sales, Product Development, Hiring, Facilities
	Batching:
		Set time aside just to:
			Gather new sales leads (once a week)
			Enter new sales leads in CRM (after gathering leads)
			Write introductory emails to prospective customers (after CRM)
				Automate (be careful)
			Follow up on previous leads and emails (weekly)
	Be strategic, don't get caught up in putting out fires
		If you're running in circles, put more processes in place and/or hire more people on!
	Don't forget, you are developing the sales process to hand off to someone else.
		Document this process
			How often do you do each thing?'

Hiring your first salesperson
	Find someone who 
		you are comfortable with representing you and your company
		is eager to help the company not just get paid
		is teachable/adaptable 
			know they could say it better / understand the tech more
		not easily offended - resilient
	Plan their onboarding
		Don't hire and immediately send out, slowly transition from you as the owner being the salesperson to a new salesperson.
			Transition for both you and the customer
				History of customer
			Introduce to most reliable customers first (who wont give them a hard time / throw a fit they don't have the owner's ear anymore)
		Train them on the process you have developed
	Make it easy for them to succeed
		When they succeed, you succeed
