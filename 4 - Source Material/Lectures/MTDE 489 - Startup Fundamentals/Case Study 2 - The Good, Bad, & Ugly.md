### Key Points:

1. **Amgen (The Good)**
   - Amgen set the gold standard for biotech companies, succeeding through rigorous scientific methods, interdisciplinary collaboration, and a focus on innovation and ethics.
   - Leadership at Amgen emphasized a strong team culture, clear communication, and shared values, helping the company scale and sustain long-term success.
   - The company adapted its processes to maintain innovation as it grew, decentralizing decision-making to stay nimble.

2. **Theranos (The Bad & Ugly)**
   - Theranos pursued revolutionary diagnostics but failed due to unethical leadership, poor processes, and deceptive practices. The lack of transparency and validation led to catastrophic failure.
   - The leadership, particularly Elizabeth Holmes, misled investors and partners with false claims about the product's capabilities. 
   - The company culture was marked by secrecy, [[Fear]], and an unwillingness to engage in scientific debate or seek expert advice, leading to a toxic environment.

3. **Leadership Lessons (3Ps)**
   - **People**: Strong leadership and ethical culture are crucial to success. Amgen thrived with collaborative, transparent leadership, while Theranos suffered from toxic and deceptive leadership.
   - **Problem**: Both companies sought to address significant problems, but only Amgen approached them with the rigorous validation needed to succeed.
   - **Processes**: Successful companies like Amgen validate their innovations with interdisciplinary collaboration and incremental testing, while Theranos ignored these processes in favor of hype and secrecy.

4. **Warren Bennis Leadership Qualities** (Exemplified by Amgen but not Theranos):
   - Shared meaning and vision.
   - Integrity and a strong moral compass.
   - Adaptability to change.
   - Mentorship and leadership development.

5. **Theranos Failure**: 
   - Bad leadership combined with flawed processes, lack of transparency, and unchecked overconfidence led to its downfall, highlighting the dangers of neglecting proper scientific and ethical practices in life sciences.

### What does Amgen actually do?

Amgen is one of the world's leading biotechnology companies, and its core focus is on developing innovative medicines based on advanced biological science. Here's what Amgen actually does:

#### 1. **Biopharmaceutical Development**:

Amgen specializes in creating biopharmaceuticals, which are drugs derived from living organisms. These are typically large, complex molecules like proteins, antibodies, or enzymes, as opposed to traditional small-molecule drugs synthesized in a lab. Their products are used to treat serious illnesses, primarily in the fields of:

- **Oncology** (cancer)
- **Hematology** (blood-related disorders)
- **Inflammatory diseases**
- **Bone health**
- **Cardiovascular disease**
- **Kidney disease**

For example, one of Amgen's first major successes was **Epogen® (epoetin alfa)**, which stimulates red blood cell production and is used to treat anemia, particularly in patients with chronic kidney disease.

#### 2. **Innovative Research and Development**:

Amgen invests heavily in **research and development (R&D)** to discover and develop new biologic therapies. They focus on understanding the complex biology of diseases to find treatments that can dramatically improve patient outcomes.

Their approach relies on advancements in **genetic engineering**, which allows them to develop therapeutic proteins and antibodies to treat diseases at their source.

#### 3. **Biomanufacturing**:

Amgen is also a leader in **biomanufacturing**, where they use genetically engineered cells to produce complex biologic medicines. This process involves growing living cells in large bioreactors and using them to create the therapeutic proteins and antibodies used in their drugs.

This capability requires highly sophisticated manufacturing infrastructure, and Amgen is known for its ability to scale this process to meet global demand.

#### 4. **Focus on Unmet Medical Needs**:

Amgen’s goal is to address **unmet medical needs**—conditions where effective treatments either don't exist or are inadequate. By targeting diseases with limited options, they strive to make a significant impact on patients' quality of life.

#### 5. **Global Reach and Distribution**:

Amgen operates globally, distributing its medicines to patients in many countries. Their products are typically prescribed by healthcare professionals and are often used in serious, chronic, or life-threatening conditions.

#### Examples of Notable Amgen Products:

- **Neupogen® (filgrastim)**: Used to prevent infections in cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy by boosting white blood cell production.
- **Enbrel® (etanercept)**: A leading treatment for autoimmune diseases like rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis.
- **Prolia® (denosumab)**: Treats osteoporosis by preventing bone loss.
- **Repatha® (evolocumab)**: A cholesterol-lowering drug that inhibits a protein involved in cholesterol metabolism.

In summary, Amgen develops biologic drugs that target complex diseases with significant unmet needs, and it leads in both the science of biotechnology and the large-scale production of biologic medicines.

### Talking Points:

1. **Amgen's Success**:
   - Importance of rigorous scientific methods in leadership and decision-making.
   - How Amgen's leadership team fostered a collaborative and ethical culture to drive innovation.
   - Amgen’s strategic processes, such as decentralizing decision-making to maintain flexibility as they grew.

2. **Theranos' Failure**:
   - The dangers of charismatic leadership without substance or accountability.
   - The impact of poor governance and lack of scientific validation on Theranos' ultimate collapse.
   - The importance of due diligence and ethical leadership in life sciences.

3. **Leadership Framework (3Ps)**:
   - Discuss the balance of **People**, **Problem**, and **Processes** for successful companies.
   - How Theranos failed to align these elements while Amgen succeeded.
