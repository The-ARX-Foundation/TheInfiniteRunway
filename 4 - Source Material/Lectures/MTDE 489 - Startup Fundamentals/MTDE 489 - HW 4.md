## 1.  What is your takeaway from case study #2 and our class discussion on ethics?

(Copied from HW 3) An unethical business practice likely won't be sustainable. In Theranos's case bad leadership combined with flawed processes, lack of transparency, and unchecked overconfidence led to its downfall, highlighting the dangers of neglecting proper scientific and ethical practices in the medical field.

## 2.  What is your takeaway from guest speaker Andrew Kazlow?  What could Mr. Kazlow do to improve his talk?  Would you recommend him as a speaker for this class next semester?

(Copied from HW 3) Mr. Kazlow's talk was genuinely super informative into the world of angel investors; his business's "Angel Ops as a Service" approach highlighted the needs of the community and how he was able to find his fit serving those needs. My biggest takeaway from his talk was not from the viewpoint of an investor, rather from someone who may be in the chair asking for funding; specifically points about what angels look at (that being the market, team, financials/ROI, traction, and how de-risked the business is) and why startups fail (that being one of the following: founder quits, they run out of cash, they lack of market fit, poor execution, co-founder conflict, pivot too late or too many pivots). I would definitely recommend him as a speaker for next semester and wouldn't change much about his talk other than potentially finding quicker ways to engage the audience other than asking for questions (was a slow build-up as we got acquainted).

## 3.  What is your takeaway from guest speaker Kevin Parakkattu?  What could Mr. Parakkattu do to improve his talk?  Would you recommend him as a speaker for this class next semester?

As an experienced venture capitalist (VC), Mr. Parakkattu delivered a comprehensive view of startup growth, market analysis, and investor strategies; as such my biggest takeaway was that of a deeper understanding of the investment community and how I might approach it one day. Outside of the main material, he also provided an outlook from the perspective of hindsight as he was recently in our shoes; he emphasized the importance of taking initiative and that success in the startup world requires self-motivation and resilience, remarking that "when you're at rock bottom, you've got nothing to lose." The only thing I wish was present was some form of visual aid; however due to the conversational nature of the presentation I know this would be significantly difficult to achieve / irrelevant. I would most definitely recommend him for next semester.

## 5.  Think about your emerging technology for this class (Used for presentations).  How would you approach the market for your idea?  Who is your end user and customer?  What type of sale would this be and what sales channels would you target?  Do some research.  I want to see links to who you would approach and an explanation as to why.

Our product is the MyoSense implantable myoelectric biosensor. Per our market analysis, we believe that the sports medicine market would be the best avenue for our product. As such, athletes and their teams (as in managers, physical trainers, etc. not necessarily the teams they play on; though that could be an avenue in and of itself) would each be both our end user and customer (athletes are the subject to be implanted, could be the person purchasing that implant, and may be the users of their data; similarly the team could be the entity purchasing the device and using the data). As the athlete would likely only get enough sensors to cover the muscles needing data, our sales model would likely fall into the direct to consumer category; however when targeting teams (as in the actual teams this time), or even leagues, it would likely fall into the business to business category due to the potential for partnerships, endorsement, and other factors that come into play in the sports industry (see Henry Schein Medical's partnership with the UFL [1] for a perfect example of this). 
Specifically, as we are just starting out, approaching early adopters of sports medicine technology (such as the University of Virginia Men’s and Women’s Lacrosse teams [2]) would prove vital to building our reputation and get customer feedback such that later on we could target the much larger mainstream audience (NFL teams or even the NFL as a whole for an example).
