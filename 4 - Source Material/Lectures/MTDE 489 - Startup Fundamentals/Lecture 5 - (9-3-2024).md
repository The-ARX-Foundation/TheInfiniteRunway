## Funding
General Funding:
	Debt:
		Loans from Banks
			Banks don't loan money to people that need it, they loan it to people who don't want to convert assets to cash.
				You need collateral
		Loans from Individuals
			Typically going to request equity / terms
	Equity:
		Ownership interest in your business 
			"Giving away your future profits"
		Angel Investors:
			High net worth individuals (personal money)
			Usually smaller amounts of money and equity
			Focused on potential for growth
		Venture Capitalists
			Pooled investors
				Raised money from other investors - not just their own money
			Focus is on companies that are about to have explosive growth
		Strategic / Corporate VC's
			Large companies
		Overseas Investors - Foreign Investors
			Biggest are China, Germany, and Saudi Arabia
				BE VERY CAREFUL
				Hella rules and regulations, get legal council
		Intermediaries:
			Investment Bankers
				Primarily concerned with raising capital for larger corporations
				Work on structuring of M&A
				IPO's
			Private Equity Firms
				Purchasing and re-selling mature companies
					Typically distressed so they can buy at discount
					Merge with other companies
					Spin-offs of larger companies
						Typically lower value companies that aren't making them much money
				Utilize debt equity so the company falls into their hands
				Not typically interested in startups
		Mergers & Acquisitions:
			Typically thought as exit plan
			If you want the idea to come to fruition ASAP can sell to larger company but keep the management role.
				Paid off based on milestones

Sources for YOUR startup:
	YOUR money :/
	Friends, Family, and Fools
	Grants (Great sources for non-dilutive funding, but lots of overhead)
		NSF I-Site
		SBIR (Small Business Innovation Research)
		SBTT (Small Business Technology Transfer)
	Bootstrapping
		"Pull yourself up"
		Pay with growth via revenue or from personal money
		YOU keep the equity
			You take the risk though
		Lack of funds = slower growth
		Utilize peripheral products / services
			Gives you customer-base / contacts
				Builds your brand / gets your name out there
			Gets you experience in the industry
				Helps you learn the problems that the customers would actually value being solved
	Crowdfunding
		Kickstarter, GoFundMe, Patreon
		Keep all your equity
	Incubators & Accelerators
		Community, resources, mentors, contacts, networking for small amounts of equity (or money)
		Incubator = early idea stage
		Accelerators are for post MVP/prototype stage
		EX: Capital Factory (Austin), Plug and Play (BCS)
	Angel Investors
		Early stage from personal money
	VC
		If showing explosive growth potential

Startup Categorization:
	Pre-seed - no valuation -> looking for 10k to 100k
		Idea phase
		working on proof of concept
	Seed - 2 mil - 5 mil -> looking for 
		pre-to-early revenue stage
		Convertible Instruments
			Convertible Note
				Short-term debt instrument that converts to equity upon a predetermined event such as future fundraising round, metric milestones, etc.
					Why? Easy terms, no valuation required, quick and simple deal
				Terms: 
					Interest rate and maturity date
						Investor has option to call loan due if event does not occur by maturity date
					Valuation Cap - max value it will convert to
					Discount rate - discount to the valuation at the priced equity round
					"Most Favored Nation" clause - always get best conversion rate
			SAFEs (Simple agreement for future equity)
				Similar to convertible note without interest rate or maturity date
					EX: 1 mil invested, value cap at 4 mil, discount rate of 80%
						if new valuation is 10 mil, they get 25% of company
						if new valuation is still 4 mil, they get 31.25% (based on discount)
					Benefits you ***if* you can grow your company to more than initial value cap**
				Terms:
					Valuation cap
					Discount rate
					"Most Favored Nation" clause  
	Series A - 5 mil to 25 mil
		Rounds of funding
		"Reaching escape velocity"
		For if you need money to set up manufacturing, to set up a sales force, etc.
		Convertible instruments are converted.
		If not a C-Corp must change charter to be one.
			For preferred shares
				Liquidation preference upon liquidation event
				Liquidation Participation
					Non-participating - paid then are done
					Full-participating - paid then get paid again (double dipping)
					Capped participation - paid then get converted to common stock capped to a certain amount.
			Issue stock option pool - to be used to attract and retain future or key employees (from founders equity)
		Protective provisions
			Prevents dilution
			Must get VC approval for 
				Selling / liquidation of company
					They get final say on terms
				Amending Certificate of Incorporation or By Laws
				Raising more capital
				Taking on debt
				Increasing shares 
				Increasing or decreasing Board of Directors
		Drag Along Rights - VC can force the company to sell
		Right of First Refusal
			Restrict the sale of company shares - Must offer to VC first
		Board Structure
			Several VC seats they get to control
			They can remove you as CEO (not necessarily as owner though)
			DO NOT TIE UP COMPANY POSITION TO BoD Seat (in case you are out of commission)
	Series B, C, D, etc. - 15 mil to 25 mil
		Expansions & acquisitions of complementary business
		Growing the company but just need money
			"The owner of a growing business has no money"
	IPO - Initial Public Offering
		Publicly traded companies
		Large scale growth and expansion
			"Going global"
